Calendar used throughout: AF = "After the Fall" (the Prismfall). The game takes place in AF 300, spring to autumn equinox.

Logline

A fifteen-year-old with no Imprint steps off the last ferry into a quarantined land where a god's broken pieces are slowly overwriting the earth. Over eighteen gyms they learn that every power in Velmora, from the eco-zealots to the energy barons to the beloved Champion, needs them at the same ceremony for opposite reasons. And the piece of god in their own chest is the reason.

One-paragraph pitch

Three hundred years ago a king tried to chain Arceus. Rather than be bound, the god broke itself apart. Its eighteen Plates fell across Velmora and its heart, the Legend Plate, shattered. The king's capital sank into the earth, and the gleaming city-state of Zenith now stands on top of it. The survivors built a civilization on the wreckage: seven kingdoms (Elowen the Evergreen Crown; Antumbra, the Kingdom Behind the Glass; Isenmar the Frozen Sea; Ashkaar the Dragonfire Dominion; Ossaria the Bone Sands; Zenith, the City Above; and Nadir, the City Below) and eighteen cities, each grown around a Plate's Imprint (endless geothermal fire, a river that runs both ways, a garden that never stops blooming). Each city is guarded by a Warden who keeps its Plate and answers to the Accord at neutral Crownhaven, not to the local king. The kings have resented that for three centuries. Now the Imprints are Bleeding past their borders, freezing towns and turning forests to glass, and the outside world has sealed Velmora's borders. You arrive on the last ferry before the quarantine closes. You're the only person anyone has met whom the Imprints won't touch. The eco-zealot Hollow want to tear the Plates out of the land. The energy conglomerate Meridian Concord wants to tap them dry. The Accord, led by Sovereign Valen Corrigan, wants everything to stay exactly as it is, and has been quietly dying to keep it that way. The Festival of Champions will bring all eighteen Plates together for the first time in a decade, and the champions of other regions are coming to watch. Everyone is waiting for you to get there. Your choice at the end decides whether Velmora keeps its miracles or finally lets its god come home.

Themes

  1. Letting go. The Plates, grief, power, a seat, a name. Every major arc ends with someone releasing something they've built their life around. Rowan can't let go of his Fixed wife. Valen can't let go of the seat. Velmora can't let go of its miracles. The good ending is literally Velmora letting go.
  2. The emergency that became permanent. The Accord's founders meant to keep the Plates for a while, until the land healed. Three hundred years later the whole economy runs on a temporary measure. Meridian industrializes the dependency (an energy-transition allegory; keep it implicit, never preachy).
  3. Inheritance. Everyone carries something handed down. Kestrel carries a disgraced name, Juno a seat she never wanted, Voss a dead king's ambition, Valen a lie three centuries old, and the protagonist a shard they never asked for. Each of them has to decide what to keep.
  4. Truth versus stability. Valen lies to keep people alive. Dorian's theater tells the truth when the throne won't. Ilsa can read every truth and chooses silence. The game never says lying is simply wrong. It shows what the lie costs.
  5. Belonging. The protagonist has no Imprint and belongs to no city. They are the only one who can carry everyone home. In the true ending they are imprinted for the first time, by the plain, ordinary land.

Tone guide

The tone curve (REVISION 2): easy to fall into, then steadily heavier. - Act I (Rejuvenation-warm): cozy Ferrow's Landing, a berry farm, a theater, a haunted manor romp, funny rivals. The Bleed is eerie but beautiful. The Lowlight Shatter at the end of Act I is the first real wound. - Act II (Reborn-grey): the cost becomes visible. Fixing wards, company towns, an act of terrorism and an unmasking that hurts someone you like. - Acts III–V (Desolation-leaning): kingdoms mobilize, a city sinks, a massacre in Nadir, a mentor framed, a rival curdles, betrayals, a Champion's slow death, and at least one named death the player can't prevent (Corvin's apprentice Wenlow, in the Sump Purge, Ch16). Hope survives because people keep choosing it, never because the game softens.

  • Mature but hopeful. Death, grief, political violence, corporate atrocity and complicity all happen on-screen. Cruelty is never gratuitous, and nobody is evil "for fun". Hope is earned and specific: people choose to do better.
  • Every antagonist is partially right. The Hollow are right that the Plates must leave. Meridian is right that the Accord hoards power and lies. Valen is right that ripping the Plates out unguided would kill thousands. Voss is right that gods have been absent landlords.
  • Wardens are people first. Every gym chapter turns on the Warden's personal problem, which is also their city's problem. The type theme is the costume, not the character.
  • Specific over generic. Name the street, the dish, the song, the dead. A Fixed child in the Lowlight district is more affecting than "many casualties".
  • Humor lives in the cast. Odessa's irreverence, Corvin's gallows wit, Dorian's theatrics, Bastian's folksiness and Juno's earnestness keep the dark from going grey. Rival banter should be fun.
  • No mustache-twirling. Voss is gracious, generous and devastatingly reasonable. The first person to be kind to you in Velmora is the final villain.
  • The protagonist is mostly silent, but dialogue choices matter and they speak in a few key scenes (see 03_CAST). Never write them as a blank. They are the person everyone is trying to use, and their choices are how they refuse to be used.
  • Fields are real. Characters talk about field effects as weather and geography: "Don't fight a fire-type on the ice in Rimeholt, kid, the lake'll drink it." Field transitions in story battles are plot events.

The full plot (one page)

Prologue / Act I: The Last Ferry (Gyms 1–4). In spring AF 300, the protagonist (PC) crosses the Hallow Strait on the Dovetail, the last civilian ferry before the Concert of Regions seals Velmora. Their mother Ines, a Velmoran exile, has just died. Her last letter sent them to Professor Imelda Larch in Ferrow's Landing. On board they meet Aurelian Voss, Meridian's charming CEO, who gives them a Meridian badge case "on the company". At the dock, Meridian's Imprint scanner reads the PC as NULL. Voss smooths it over and starts watching. Larch gives the PC a starter. Juno Hale, Larch's sunny assistant, and Kestrel Vane, the angry grandson of the lighthouse keeper, take theirs (K1). The PC learns they can walk into Bleed zones that make everyone else sick. The road through Elowen shows the cost of the Imprints and of Meridian's extraction Lattices. In Briarwick, the young Bug Warden secretly signed with Meridian. In Saltmere, the Crown's free port, the masked Mourner of the Hollow hijacks the official Prismfall pageant. Across the border in Isenmar, the Water Warden of Ebbmouth is caught between the Thane's taxes, Meridian's jobs and a brother who runs a Hollow cell. The act ends at Meridian's gala in Lumen City, capital district of Zenith, whose sewers run down into Nadir. A Hollow raid on the Zap Plate goes wrong: the Plate Shatters and twelve people in the Lowlight district are turned to glass. The PC sees the Mourner holding the cracked Plate with a burning hand. Voss restores the light. Sovereign Valen arrives to grant Meridian emergency security powers.

Act II: Imprints (Gyms 5–8). Larch suspects the Plate was already cracking under Meridian's drain. In Ossaria, Duneholt's shifting sands uncover Unown ruins and murals of a golden king chaining a beast of eighteen lights. Down in Nadir, Mirefen's Poison Warden runs a ward of Fixing patients in Zenith's toxic runoff, and Juno finds her uncle Rowan's name in his ledger. In Ashkaar, a Meridian geothermal tap nearly blows Cindervault's caldera, and the Dragon-Lord threatens war on Zenith. The Hollow are fracturing: the Mourner now wants to return the Plates, while a radical called Sable wants more Shatters. At the Warden Conclave in Ironreach's pit arena, Sable tries to Shatter the Fist Plate. The Mourner shields the crowd, and his mask breaks: he is Rowan Ashby, the Grass Warden, Juno's beloved uncle. Meridian takes him into custody. Valen watches the PC stand unharmed in the Bleed burst and says: "Come to Verdance. There's a seat to fill."

Act III: Fault Lines (Gyms 9–12). Verdance, Elowen's royal seat, has no Warden and is drowning in runaway bloom. Elowen's Queen claims the Meadow Plate for her crown, Meridian offers "custody", and Valen backs Juno, who inherits the seat (J3). The Accord splits into blocs over Meridian's Mandate while the kingdoms arm. In Isenmar, Rimeholt's lake thaws under a Meridian Lattice and wakes the Kyogre frozen beneath it. In Skyreach, Zenith's airship spire, Larch publishes proof that extraction feeds the Bleed and is framed for it. Kestrel, now taking Meridian's sponsorship, steals a bundle of dead letters, one of them from his mother to "I." The PC crosses the Veil into Antumbra, the mirrored Reverse Kingdom where Giratina stirs. In Lucent, the Psychic Warden Ilsa (Valen's estranged daughter) takes the PC into the Mindscape. They recover a memory: at the last Festival, ten years ago, when the PC was five, the Sovereign's Crown cracked and something went into them. At Rowan's tribunal, Sable is exposed as a Meridian operative: the Hollow's worst atrocities were Voss's false flags. Then Meridian test-fires its secret engine in Cogsworth. A continental tremor, the Fault, pulls the sea back from the north coast overnight, draining the Sump beneath Nadir and exposing the spires of drowned Aurelia under Zenith's cliffs, below Underveil.

Act IV: The Drowned Crown (Gyms 13–16). The truth comes out in layers. Bastian, the oldest Warden, saw Meridian technicians at the Crown when it cracked. In Gravenmoor's Hollow Cathedral, the Ghost Imprint replays the AF 290 Fracture (K6). Kestrel's mother, Ghost Warden Morwen Vane, shielded the five-year-old PC, smuggled them out of Velmora and took the blame. Valen let her be ruined to keep the ceremony's secret. In Cogsworth, the PC finds the blueprints of the Arbiter Engine, an artificial Judgment built into the new Festival arena. It needs eighteen Plates, a keystone and "Subject UNIMPRINTED-01". Meridian's Mandate troops "pacify" Nadir in the Sump Purge, and Zenith's topside watches it on the news. Beneath Underveil, in the drowned capital Aurelia that Zenith was built on, the murals tell the real Prismfall. Voss stands in the Gilded King's throne room as that king's heir and names what the PC carries: a splinter of the Legend Plate, Arceus's heart. Their badge case has been recording the shard's resonance for Voss with every badge.

Act V: Sovereign (Gyms 17–18 + Festival). In Gloaming, at the heart of Elowen's forest, the Fairy Warden gives the PC the suppressed Hymn of Return: the founders always meant to give the Plates back. Kestrel (K7) tries to stop the PC from going to the Festival, because he finally understands that everyone wants to use them. In Ashkaar's Wyrmrest, Valen's chosen heir Sigrun, the Dragon-Lord's own daughter, makes the PC prove they are choosing this with open eyes. Champions from other worlds (Reborn, Aevium, Ayrith) arrive through Antumbra's Veil Gate. The Festival opens with all eighteen Plates and the Tuning, and the PC's shard sees Arceus. Over the week the PC beats Juno, Lin, Reborn's Meteor Champion, who once caught a god herself, and Kestrel. Kestrel tears off his Meridian gauntlet mid-battle and finally reads his mother's letter. Valen confesses everything: when the PC beats him, he will crown them and their shard will make the Crown whole. The PC will hold the Plates quiet for a century, alone. The PC beats Valen. At the coronation, Voss triggers the Arbiter Engine and the Meridian Prism Arena becomes his weapon. The PC defeats him, but the Engine drags Arceus down in golden chains. Valen holds the containment ring with his own body while the PC battles the bound, maddened god through all eighteen types. Valen burns out as Arceus is freed. Arceus offers the PC a choice: return the Plates (the Imprints fade, the Bleed heals, the Fixed who still dream wake up, and Velmora must learn to live without miracles) or keep them and wear the Crown for a hundred years.

Core mysteries and their answers

Mystery Answer Revealed
Why is the PC Unimprinted? A splinter of the Legend Plate lodged in them as a five-year-old when the Sovereign's Crown cracked at the AF 290 Festival. The Legend Plate holds every type and none, so Imprints wash through them without marking them. Hinted from the Prologue; memory in Ch13 (Lucent); named in Ch17 (Underveil)
Who is the Mourner? Rowan Ashby, Grass Warden of Verdance and Juno's uncle by marriage. His wife Elin was Fixed by the Bleed into the flowering "Bride Tree" twelve years ago. Unmasked end of Act II (Ironreach)
What is the Bleed? The Plates trying to reconverge. Each Imprint is a Plate reaching for the others. Bleed zones are where the reaching overlaps, which is why the :BLEED field borrows random effects from other fields. Theory from Larch (Act II); confirmed by the Hymn (Act V)
What does the Festival ceremony really do? The Tuning. The Sovereign wears the Crown, which holds the largest surviving Legend shard, and hushes the converged Plates for another ten years. The bearer's body pays the cost. Since the Crown cracked, it has been killing Valen: his left arm is crystal under the glove. Hinted Act I; confirmed Act IV (Brann/Bastian), confessed Act V
Why was Morwen Vane disgraced? Valen scapegoated her for the AF 290 "Glassfall" (the Bleed burst when the Crown cracked) to protect the ceremony's secret. She took the blame silently to protect the PC. Ch15 (Gravenmoor, K6)
What really happened in the Prismfall? King Theodric Aurel ("the Gilded King") tried to bind Arceus with the Gilded Chain. Arceus shed its Plates to escape, and the shockwave collapsed the capital Aurelia into a flooded abyss, the Sump. In AF 118, guild-houses founded Zenith on top of it. One of those houses, the Vosses, was secretly the Gilded King's line. His daughter Maren founded the Accord as penance. Duneholt Unown murals (Act II) → "The Gilded King" play → Underveil (Act IV)
Who cracked the Crown in AF 290? Meridian. A young Aurelian Voss's "monitoring array" drew on the converged Plates as a proof-of-concept for the Arbiter Engine. Bastian (Ch14); echo (Ch15); Voss admits it (Ch17/Festival)
What does Voss want? To finish his ancestor's work: forge an artificial Judgment and chain Arceus as a permanent power source. He sincerely believes humanity should never depend on an absent god again. Act IV
What does Valen want for the PC? To crown them as the new, nearly unbreakable bearer of the Tuning, a living lock for a century. Act V audience
Who is the Hollow's radical, Sable? Sabine Kroll, a Meridian Special Accounts operative. The Lumen Shatter and the Ironreach attack were Voss's false flags, staged to win the Mandate and to collect Shatter data for the Engine. Ch13 (Lucent tribunal)
Why are Velmorans unable to leave? Imprinted people slowly sicken far from their Imprint ("wilting"). The PC's mother wilted in exile for ten years to keep them hidden. Ch1 hint; Ch12 letters; Ch15
What do the Plates want? To go home. The founders' Hymn of Return says a "child of no color" would one day walk the eighteen roads and carry them home. Valen reads it as "a vessel"; Celestine reads it as "a walker". Act V

Story Bible 01: World Lore — Velmora

Geography follows design/WORLD.md §1 (World lead). Kingdom names are final per SPINE Revision 3. Calendar: AF = After the Fall.

1. Cosmology in one breath

Arceus, the Original One, shaped the world and keeps it in balance through its eighteen Plates, one for each type. At its heart sits a nineteenth, the Legend Plate, which holds every type and none. The Plates are not tools Arceus carries. They are Arceus, the way a heartbeat is part of a body. Separate them and Arceus becomes incomplete: it sleeps, wounded and unable to act, somewhere beyond the sky that Velmoran priests call the Hall of First Light.

For three hundred years Velmora has lived on pieces of a sleeping god.

2. History

Before the Fall: the Aureate Kingdom

Before AF 0, one kingdom ruled the continent: the Aureate Kingdom of House Aurel. Its capital, Aurelia, rose in gold terraces up the northern sea-cliffs. The Aurels were builders and believers. Their court artificers, the Gilders, combined clockwork, alchemy and old Pokémon pacts into something close to science.

The kingdom's last two decades were the Blight Years: crop-rot, a wasting plague and three failed harvests running. King Theodric Aurel, later called the Gilded King, tried to fix the world by force. - First he tried to remake the land. The Gilders woke Groudon beneath the Bone Sands and Kyogre off the northern coast. The two titans nearly tore the continent in half before they were driven back to sleep. Ossaria's Unown records call this "the Year of Two Weathers". - Then he went higher. The Gilders forged the Gilded Chain: eighteen golden locks, one for each Plate, around a keystone cradle shaped for the Legend Plate. They meant to summon Arceus and bind it to the throne. "If heaven will not kneel, we'll build a chain." — Theodric, as quoted in "The Gilded King".

Theodric was not a monster. His people were starving, and he loved them. That is the point, and it is why Voss can quote him without irony.

AF 0: the Prismfall

At midsummer, in the Sun Court of Aurelia, the Gilders performed the Calling. Arceus descended, and the Chain closed. For one heartbeat the god was bound.

Then Arceus did the only thing a god can do that a chain cannot hold: it broke itself. - The Plates. It shed all eighteen Plates in a burst of light seen from every corner of the continent. They scattered across the land and buried themselves where they struck. Every Plate began to Imprint the ground around it with its type. - The Legend Plate, caught in the keystone cradle, shattered. - Aurelia. The shockwave collapsed the city into the cliffs. The sea poured into the ruins, and Aurelia became the Drowned Crown. - Isenmar. The surge rolled west along the coast and woke Kyogre. At that moment the Icicle Plate fell into the waves and froze Kyogre and the sea around it solid. That frozen sea is Isenmar. - Antumbra. Behind the great forest of the south, the world tore. A mirrored reverse side opened behind the Glass. Giratina, the guardian of reverse places, came through to hold the seam closed. - Arceus, incomplete, fled beyond the sky and slept.

AF 0–4: the Grey Years

Survivors gathered around the fallen Plates because the Plates kept them alive. Fire that never went out. Water that never froze. Fields that bloomed in a week. Cities grew around each Imprint. Families who kept the Plates safe from raiders became the first Wardens.

AF 4: the Concordance

Theodric's daughter Princess Maren Aurel had opposed the Calling and survived the Fall. She pulled the largest surviving shard of the Legend Plate from the wreck of the keystone cradle. She renounced her house, took the name Maren Ilse, and climbed the high caldera at the center of the continent, where all eighteen Imprints can be seen glowing on a clear night.

There she gathered the eighteen Plate-keeping families and the war-chiefs of the provinces that would become the kingdoms. Together they signed the Concordance, founding the Accord. The caldera city became Crownhaven.

Public articles (taught in every schoolroom): 1. Every Plate is held in trust for all Velmora by its Warden. No king may own or seize a Plate. 2. Wardens answer to the Accord, not to the crown of the land they stand on. 3. Once each decade, the Plates are carried to Crownhaven for the Festival, and the strongest trainer in the realm holds the Sovereign Seat. 4. Motto: "Eighteen in Concord, One in Keeping." (Everyone thinks "One" means the Sovereign. It means the Legend Plate.)

Secret articles (the Sovereign, the Hierophant, the Underveil Keepers and Gloaming's Warden alone): - The Tuning. Left alone, the Plates reach for each other. This is the Bleed. Maren set her Legend shard into the Sovereign's Crown. Every ten years, with all eighteen Plates gathered, the Crown-bearer hushes them for another decade. - The Hymn of Return. The founders swore the Plates were borrowed. "When the land is healed, one of no color shall walk the eighteen roads, and carry them home." Keeping the Plates was meant to be temporary.

3. How Imprints work

In-world science, per Larch's Field Notes (Rev. 3 adds an in-game Field Notes encyclopedia, and these are her entries' voice): - The Imprint. A Plate saturates the land around it with its type, typically within 5–15 km and strongest at the heart. Matter, weather and Pokémon behavior bend toward that type. This is not metaphor. Field effects are Imprints: fire really does burn hotter in Cindervault, sound really does carry on Saltmere's Grand Stage, and the ice really does drink heat in Rimeholt. Battles fought in an Imprint obey it. - Resources. Each Imprint is an economy: Cindervault's ever-fire, the Ebb's two-way current, Verdance's endless harvest, Briarwick's silk that never tangles, Lumen City's free current, and so on. - Tint. People and Pokémon raised in an Imprint carry a faint affinity for its type. Velmorans call it their tint, as in "she's Cindervault-tinted, she'll run warm." Tint shows on Meridian scanners as a color bar. Foreigners take on a tint after a few weeks. The PC never does. - Eevee evolves according to the Imprint it is raised in. Velmorans call it "the Plate's child", and an Eevee is a traditional gift to a newborn. (This is the lore behind the Eevee Octave.) - Seeds. Imprints condense into seeds in the right soil. Elemental, Magical and Telluric Seeds grow wild at Imprint borders and are harvested by seed-pickers. Synthetic Seeds are Meridian's lab-grown version, sold in every Mart since AF 294. - Terrain. Electric, Grassy, Misty and Psychic Terrain are "small, temporary Imprints a Pokémon makes itself". Where they meet a real Imprint they layer over it, are smothered by it, or transform it. Locals know which, and NPCs should say so.

4. The Bleed

  • What it is: the Plates reaching for each other. Each Imprint is slowly extending toward the others, and where two or more overlap, reality loses its grip on what type it is. In battle, that is the :BLEED field: unstable, with effects borrowed at random from other fields. It has accelerated sharply since AF 289, because Meridian's Lattices draw on Imprints like a pump draws on a well, and a drained Imprint reaches harder.
  • Bleed zones look beautiful at first: glass orchards, forests of frozen birdsong, rivers hanging in the air. They are deadly to anyone with a tint. Every zone has a Bleed Veil at its edge, a shimmer in the air that tinted people physically cannot cross ("my skin's burning, I can't"). The PC walks through.
  • Fixing: prolonged Bleed exposure Fixes a person into the substance of the overlapping Imprints. It goes in stages: 1. Glassy fingernails. 2. "Sleepwalking": staring, humming. 3. Partial Fixing: crystal, ice, bark or salt spreads from the extremities. 4. Full Fixing.

The Fixed are not dead. They dream, and with time some stop dreaming. Corvin's Quiet Ward in Mirefen holds forty patients at stages 2–3. The Accord hides whole Fixed towns: the Quiet Towns (Marrowgate, Lowmarsh, Halden's Reach East). - Wilting: a tinted person far from their Imprint slowly sickens with weakness, grey skin and grief. This is why Velmorans almost never emigrate, and why the outside world's quarantine is cruel twice over. The PC's mother wilted in exile for ten years to keep her child hidden. - The Shatter: strike a Plate hard enough and it releases its Imprint all at once. The result is a Bleed explosion and instant Fixing within a few hundred meters. The Lumen Shatter (Act I) Fixed twelve people in the Lowlight district.

5. The Tuning and the Crown

  • The Sovereign's Crown is a plain silver circlet holding a single colorless stone, the largest surviving shard of the Legend Plate.
  • The Tuning. At the Festival's opening ceremony, the Wardens set their Plates into the eighteen pylons of the Concordance Ring in the arena. The Choir sings, and the Crown-bearer hushes the Plates' reaching. To the public it is a lightshow. In truth, the bearer's body is the conduit.
  • The cost. The Crown's shard used to carry most of the load. It depletes a little every decade, and each Sovereign pays more than the last. Most Sovereigns since AF 140 have died within twenty years of their first Tuning, of what the Accord calls "the Sovereign's fatigue".
  • AF 290. When Meridian's array cracked the Crown, a splinter flew free into the PC. Brann reset the stone, but the Crown has been incomplete ever since. Valen has carried nearly the whole Tuning in his body: his left arm is crystal to the shoulder, under the grey glove.
  • The ceremony is failing, and Valen knows it. He cannot survive the AF 300 Tuning without help. With the PC's splinter rejoined, the Crown would be whole, and its bearer could hold the Plates quiet for a century, alone.

6. The Other Face of the Legend Plate (Resonance: Harmony ↔ Ruin)

The Legend Plate holds every type, and none. It has two faces, and the PC's choices tilt their shard toward one of them. This is the in-world meaning of the Resonance track (SPINE Rev. 3). - Harmony (every): the shard calms. Bleed-maddened Pokémon settle near the PC, Bleed zones go quiet around them, and other Plates "hum along". This face makes Return possible. - Ruin (none): the shard unmakes. Imprints thin to nothing where the PC stands, and a Plate touched in anger cracks. At its extreme it can unmake Judgment, the Glass, or the Plates themselves. Celestine's rhyme for it: "the color that eats colors". - Story rules: - Ruin is never "evil mode". It's the face that answers despair, the Hollow's grief in its purest form. - Wardens and Pokémon feel the PC's Resonance. Corvin: "You've gone cold, love." Celestine: "You're getting louder in the wrong key." - The Ruin Route (Narrative Systems spec) follows a PC who has seen every way the world ends (the Ledger) and decides to stop asking it to be saved.

7. Religion

  • The Prism Faith (Church of the First Light). Velmora's established faith. Arceus is "the Original One". The Benediction Doctrine holds that the Prismfall was a gift of love, which is the Accord's official myth.
  • The Prism Choir, led by Hierophant Maud Ellery, performs the Tuning liturgy without knowing what it really does.
  • Churches have eighteen colored windows around a clear nineteenth pane. Prayers end with "and keep the One".
  • The Wound Doctrine (heresy). The Plates are Arceus's wound, and Velmora is living off a god's blood. Taught by Brother Wick (defrocked) and radicalized by the Hollow into "pull the nails".
  • The Gloaming Liturgy. Gloaming's dusk-chapels still sing the complete Hymn of Return in its original form. The Choir thinks it's a quaint local variant.
  • Antumbra's Veiled Faith. Giratina is "the Warden of the Reverse", the god's own night watchman. The Veiled Synod's seers read reflections, and lantern-priests guide the dead.
  • Ashkaar's Fire-Creed. The Dragon-Lords claim descent from the white dragon of truth (Reshiram). Oaths sworn over flame are binding, so lying in a Dominion court is a capital crime.
  • Isenmar's sea-songs are about "the Sleeping Whale under the ice" (Kyogre). Fishermen pour a cup of warm broth through an ice-hole before every season.
  • Nadir's folk-saints: "the Ogre in the Mask", a protector of the forgotten borrowed from Elowen's legend, painted on sump walls beside the Hollow's ring sigil.

8. Economy

  • The Imprint economy is three centuries old. Each Warden city trades its Imprint's product, and the kingdoms tax the trade, though the Concordance forbids them from taxing the Plate itself. The kings call that "the Accord's theft".
  • Meridian Concord changed everything in a decade:
  • Lattices are extraction pylons (Larch's design) that draw energy from an Imprint. Fourteen of eighteen cities have one by Act III.
  • Prism Cells are batteries charged from Lattices. Every lamp in Zenith runs on them. Under a special quarantine exemption, Meridian exports them to the Concert of Regions, so Velmora's suffering powers other people's cities.
  • Royalties. Meridian pays cities and crowns a cut, so Wardens and kings alike depend on it. Imprint industries that compete with Meridian (Ebbmouth's tidal mills, Briarwick's looms) are bought out or starved.
  • The Mandate (from Act II on): after the Lowlight Shatter, the Accord grants Meridian Security police powers wherever Lattices stand.
  • Nadir's economy:
  • Prism slag is Lattice runoff that drains down from Zenith. Mirefen's marsh is made of it.
  • The pits are Ironreach's betting arenas.
  • Sweetrot is the street chem, and the Sump Barons control it.
  • Information is Nyx's trade.

The City Below has no seat on the Zenith Council and pays Zenith's taxes anyway. - Money: the Pokédollar (₽) is standard. Old-timers in Ossaria still quote prices in "bones", fossil-shell tokens.

9. Politics: the Accord versus the kingdoms

  • The tension in one line: every king rules land with a Plate in it that they're forbidden to touch.
  • The Accord Council (meets in Crownhaven): the Sovereign, the eighteen Wardens and the Chancellor (Ambrose Hollis), who runs the bureaucracy and the Wardguard budget. Votes are by Warden. Kings attend only as "honored observers", and they hate it.
  • Blocs (from Act III, "Fault Lines"):
  • Greenward (limit extraction, oppose the Mandate): Juno, Marisol, Kaito, Corvin, Tamsin, Ysolde, Celestine.
  • Concord bloc (pro-Meridian): Magnus, Rook (until Act IV), Anja (until the Thaw), and Dorian (publicly, under protest).
  • Sovereign loyalists: Brann, Sigrun, Bastian, Sahar.
  • Independents: Odessa, Nyx, Ilsa.
  • The kingdoms' agendas:
  • Elowen wants its Plates back as Crown property.
  • Isenmar wants heat and debt relief.
  • Ashkaar wants the Draco Plate and the Flame Plate under the Dominar, and Zenith humbled.
  • Ossaria sells whatever Meridian will buy.
  • Antumbra wants the Glass kept closed and Giratina undisturbed.
  • Zenith wants the Mandate everywhere.
  • Nadir wants to exist: recognition, a vote, clean air.
  • Outside:
  • The Concert of Regions (the neighboring realms across the Hallow Strait) imposed the quarantine and will decide at this Festival whether to lift it or declare Severance, permanent abandonment. Its envoy is Delphine Okoro.
  • The Veil Gate through Antumbra is the only other door, and it opens to other worlds (see design/CAMEOS.md).

11. Places

Crownhaven (neutral; the Accord's capital; story-gated, Act V)

Built on the inner slopes of the Crown, the great caldera ring at the realm's center. Its eighteen gates face the eighteen Imprints. Upper Crownhaven is white stone, bell towers and the Basilica of First Light, and the Meridian Prism Arena (newly rebuilt, and too new) sits where the old Festival grounds were. Lower Crownhaven is the crowded crater-floor town that still has a scar: the Glassfall Scar, a district-sized stretch of glass where forty people were Fixed in AF 290. The Accord fenced it and planted a memorial garden around it, and nobody tends the garden. The Concordance Spire, the old Accord archive tower, becomes the post-game Battle Tower.

Elowen, the Evergreen Crown

The great forest of the south and south-west. An old monarchy under Queen Maelis Thorne. Wild types: Fairy, Grass, Bug, Normal. Character: storybook beauty with old rot underneath, a kingdom of fairy tales that are mostly lies. - Ferrow's Landing (start): Elowen's southern ferry dock on the Hallow Strait. A fishing village of tarred stilt-houses and drying kelp under a red-and-white lighthouse, where old Hesper Vane keeps the lamp and young Kestrel keeps out of sight. Tidewatch Station, Larch's field station and the Wayfarer Sanctuary, sits on the headland. Hale's Bramble berry farm is up the lane. The bronze Accord plaque at the dock reads "Eighteen in Concord, One in Keeping". After the last ferry, a Meridian customs gate stands beside it. - Briarwick (Gym 1, Bug, :SILKWOOD): a weaving village inside the Gossamer Wood, where the Insect Plate makes Bug Pokémon spin silk that never tangles or rots. Houses are lashed between tree trunks, and the town looms clack all day. A shrine at the village edge honors the Loyal Three, who "drove the Gossamer Ogre from the wood". Children wear carved ogre masks at Fallmorn to scare it off. The Glasswood, a crystallizing Bleed, is creeping toward the silk-moth groves from the east, along the line of Meridian's new Lattice. - Saltmere (Gym 2, Normal, :GRAND_STAGE): Elowen's chartered free port on a salt lake at the forest's coastal edge. Here the Blank Plate's Imprint is ordinariness: nothing strange ever happens in Saltmere, and Bleed never touches it. So the city built its own miracles, and it is Velmora's theater capital. The Lantern Theatre on the salt-flat promenade stages the Accord's official Pageant of the Prismfall every Fallmorn. The free-port charter means Saltmere answers to no king, and the Queen's tax-barges are turned back at the breakwater by tradition. Nearby: Thistledown Manor, home of a dead acting family who never stopped performing (the comic haunted house). - Verdance (Gym 9, Grass, :FLOWER_GARDEN): Elowen's royal seat. A green city of terraces and glasshouses where the Meadow Plate makes everything bloom constantly. Verdance has never had an autumn. Queen Maelis holds court in Briar Hall. Below it lies the Warden's Heartgarden, where the Bride Tree stands in permanent blossom. Underneath, the soil is grey and starving, and nothing rots, so nothing returns to the earth. The Royal Chase, the Queen's game reserve, sprawls outside the walls. - Gloaming (Gym 17, Fairy, :FAIRY_TALE): the forest's heart, where the Pixie Plate in the roots of the Great Tree holds the sky at permanent dusk. Lantern-moths, mushroom bridges, and a town of bargains and courtesies. Gloaming folk can't lie, age slowly, and keep the old songs. The approach is through the Gloamwood. By Act V, the dusk is spreading south across Elowen like a stain.

Antumbra, the Kingdom Behind the Glass

A mirrored realm behind Elowen's forest, entered through mirror gates such as Mirror Mere. The Reverse Kingdom is governed by the Veiled Synod under Archseer Imre Dask, and it is Giratina's domain. Wild types: Ghost, Psychic, Dark. Inverse rules (:INVERSE) hold in the borderlands: rivers run uphill, shadows fall toward the light, and type matchups flip. - Lucent (Gym 12, Psychic, :MINDSCAPE): a scholars' city that exists twice. The living city is reflected upside-down in the sky above it, and the Mind Plate lives in the reflection. The Lucent Athenaeum holds the Accord's archive, the sealed stacks, the Concordance original and the Secret Articles. The Accord holds its tribunals here, under Ilsa Marr's provostship. - Gravenmoor (Gym 14, Ghost, :HOLLOW_CATHEDRAL): a moorland cathedral town of fog, barrows and lanterns, where the Spooky Plate makes the dead linger as echoes. The Hollow Cathedral replays the last moments of anyone who died near it. Vane Hall, the Vane family manor and Kestrel's childhood home, stands empty on the hill, dust-sheeted and haunted. The Lantern Hospice is where Morwen died.

Isenmar, the Frozen Sea

The west and north coast, a sea frozen solid since AF 0 with a hard clan-kingdom built on it, under Thane Eirik Sorrel. Wild types: Water, Ice. Character: endurance, debt, and stubborn warmth. - Ebbmouth (Gym 3, Water, :WATER_SURFACE): Isenmar's southern port, where the glacier-fed River Ebb meets the open sea. The Splash Plate makes the Ebb run both ways, inland on the morning tide and out at night, and three centuries of tidal mills grew up around it. It is fog, gulls, smoked-eel stalls and union halls. The Ebb Guild elects the Warden. Meridian's tidal Lattice at the river mouth is draining the current, and the river has started to "stutter", stopping dead for minutes at a time. Nearby: Ebb Hollow, a Bleed zone where river-water hangs in the air in still sheets. - Rimeholt (Gym 10, Ice, :FROZEN_LAKE): the northern ice-city built on Lake Rime, part of the frozen sea, frozen solid for three hundred years above the sleeping Kyogre. The houses are timber and whalebone, linked by sled-roads, and ice-fishers cut holes over a darkness that sometimes breathes. Meridian's Lattice heats the city and thins the ice. In Act III, a quarter of the city sinks.

Zenith, the City Above

A gleaming city-state on the north-central plateau, all brass, glass, airship masts and progress. It is governed by the Zenith Council of founding houses, chaired by Aurelian Voss. Meridian's home. Wild types: Electric, Steel, Flying. Character: optimism with the bill sent downstairs. - Lumen City (Gym 4, Electric, :NEON_GRID): Zenith's capital district, a neon metropolis lit by the Zap Plate's free current plus Meridian's Lattice grid. Streets of advertising holograms, Rotom delivery drones and rooftop gardens. Meridian Spire rises from the cliff edge on the foundations of the old Aurelian sun tower, with its black Dark Stone core glowing in its heart. The Lowlight is the working-class district where the Spire's shadow falls. After the Shatter, part of it is glass. - Skyreach (Gym 11, Flying, :STORMFRONT): Zenith's airship spire, a vertical city of docking masts and skybridges climbing into the Stormfront, the Sky Plate's permanent storm wall. Home of the Gale Post courier guild and of every smuggler worth hiring. Stormwatch Spire at its crown holds the relays that broadcast to the whole realm. - Cogsworth (Gym 15, Steel, :FACTORY): Zenith's foundry district on the plateau's eastern edge, where the Iron Plate makes metal eager to be shaped. Smokestacks and clock-towers, three shifts a day. Magnus Strade's Strade Works builds everything from airship hulls to, secretly, the Arbiter Engine. The bathysphere works that build Dive suits for the drowned coast are here too.

Nadir, the City Below

The slums, sumps and old mine shafts beneath Zenith's cliffs, built in the caverns and in drowned Aurelia's upper towers. Zenith claims it, but it has no recognized government: Sump Barons, pit-bosses and Nyx Calloway run it. Wild types: Poison, Dark, Fighting. Character: rage, solidarity and the best street food in Velmora. - Mirefen (Gym 6, Poison, :CORROSIVE_MIRE): a stilt-town in the runoff marsh where Zenith's Lattice slag drains into the Toxic Plate's mire. The water glows faintly blue at night, which is the "poison-blue moon" of the rhyme. Fenfolk are tinted against poison and almost nothing else. Corvin Mott's Infirmary and its Quiet Ward stand on the highest stilts. - Ironreach (Gym 8, Fighting, :COLOSSEUM): the pit-fighting quarter carved into the Aurel-era mine shafts. The Fist Plate makes people and Pokémon strong, so for centuries Nadir's miners have settled everything with their hands. The Iron Pit, a colosseum in a mined-out cavern, hosts the Iron Trial and the Ironreach Open. In Act II, the Accord's Conclave is held here in the shafts under Zenith's feet, to show the City Below the Accord still cares. It goes badly. - Underveil (Gym 16, Dark, :NEW_MOON): the heart of Nadir, a city of lanterns and smugglers in the upper towers of drowned Aurelia, half-submerged in the sea-flooded Sump beneath the north cliffs. The Dread Plate keeps it in permanent night. It's not on Accord maps ("sixteen unknown"). Nyx Calloway's Moon Market trades in secrets. In Act III, the Fault drains the Sump overnight and exposes the Sun Court, the Gilded King's throne room, for the first time in 300 years.

Ashkaar, the Dragonfire Dominion

The east and south-east volcanic highlands. A feudal warrior-kingdom of dragon-lords under Dominar Hrothgar Ashfall. Wild types: Fire, Dragon. Character: honor, oaths, and a war it badly wants. - Cindervault (Gym 7, Fire, :VOLCANIC): a forge-city inside the caldera of Mount Cinder, where the Flame Plate keeps the magma at the perfect working heat. Terraced smithies, lava-channels for quenching and the Hearthguild of smiths. The guild vault holds the Light Stone, which the Dominar wants for his throne. Meridian's geothermal tap is overdrawing the caldera. Quench the Imprint and it becomes :ASHEN_WASTE. - Wyrmrest (Gym 18, Dragon, :DRAGONS_DEN): the dragon-aerie fortress on the peaks that guard the only overland pass into the Crown. Dragon-riders patrol the Wyrmscar Rift. The Draco Plate sleeps in the Rift's heart under Sigrun Ashfall's guard, while her father's legions camp on the slopes below.

Ossaria, the Bone Sands

The north-east desert and canyon country: fossil beds, caravan houses and deep mines. Ruled by Prince-Elector Idris Kassan. Wild types: Ground, Rock. Character: patience, memory, and things buried for a reason. - Duneholt (Gym 5, Ground, :DESERT): a caravan city of sandstone terraces around a deep aquifer, which the Earth Plate keeps inside the stone so the desert above stays fertile. The dunes move, and every few years they uncover something. Sahar's Dig Lab (fossil revival) is here, and the Ossuary of Dunes (Unown ruins) is on the road out. THE HOLLOW

"Let the land grieve." Eco-zealots who believe the Plates are nails driven into Velmora's body and must be pulled out. They are partially right: the Plates must leave. They are wrong about how: breaking a Plate Shatters it.

Iconography and culture

  • Grey cowls. Carved bark masks modeled on the Gossamer Ogre of Elowen folklore, the misunderstood guardian. The sigil is the Hollow Ring, an empty circle "for the space the Plates should leave".
  • Members call the Plates "the Nails". Rank-and-file call themselves the Bereaved, and almost every one of them has lost someone to the Bleed. Admins are Pallbearers. The leader is the Mourner.
  • Rituals: they leave bare soil at every site they hit, "so something can finally rot". They sing dirges at Lattice pylons.
  • Recruiting ground: Nadir, above all Mirefen and Ironreach, where Bleed-sickness and Zenith's runoff hit hardest. Sump Barons quietly fund them because anything that hurts Meridian hurts Zenith.
  • Battle style: Grass, Ghost, Dark and Poison. Bereaved grunts field "mourning" teams of Pokémon rescued from Bleed zones, some visibly glassed (a palette swap only, no mechanic).

Why Rowan Ashby is the Mourner (justification)

  1. Theme. The man who loves the land most is the one who sees the Imprint as a sickness. Rowan has spent 24 years in a garden that never stops blooming over soil that never gets to rest. "The flowers never die here. Do you know what that means? Nothing rots. Nothing returns."
  2. Motive. His grief is literal and visible. His wife Elin is the Bride Tree, and he waters her every dawn. Every Hollow speech is a eulogy for her.
  3. Access. He is a Warden. He has attended three Tunings, knows the ceremony is more than a lightshow, and was told by Valen to "tend his garden". He knows where Plates are kept and how Wardens think.
  4. Structure. SPINE locks Juno's succession to the Grass seat in Act III. Rowan's unmasking at the end of Act II is the cleanest, most painful reason the seat falls vacant. It also turns the friendly rival's story into the emotional engine of Act III.
  5. A fair twist. Clues are planted from Ch1 (see 09). Misdirection points elsewhere: the realm-wide rumor that the Mourner is Kestrel's dead mother, and Kestrel's own obsessive belief in it.
  6. Sympathy. He never wanted deaths. The Lowlight Shatter (Sable's doing) horrifies him and drives him from "pull the nails" toward Wick's "carry them home". That pivot is the Hollow's arc in miniature.

How the Hollow changes across the acts

  • Act I: theater and sabotage. They cut Meridian survey lines in Briarwick, hijack Saltmere's pageant and spike Ebbmouth's tidal Lattice. The tone is romantic outlaws. At the Lumen Gala, the Mourner tries to steal the Zap Plate from Meridian's vault to stop the Lattice drain. Sable detonates a Splitter on it, the Lowlight Shatters, and the realm blames the Hollow. The Mourner's hand is burned catching the cracked Plate.
  • Act II: the split. Rowan pulls back and starts hunting for "the other way" (the Legend Plate, the Hymn). Sable gathers the angriest Bereaved into the Hammer, a faction that wants more Shatters "until they listen". At Cindervault they compete openly: Wick's people try to vent the caldera safely while the Hammer tries to blow it. At the Ironreach Conclave the Hammer tries to Shatter the Fist Plate under the Wardens' feet. The Mourner shields the crowd and is unmasked and arrested.
  • Act III: leaderless and framed.
  • The Hammer carries out a string of "Hollow atrocities". Some are real. Most are Meridian false flags, including the Skyreach relay bombing blamed on Larch.
  • Wick holds the faithful together in hiding: the Hollow Sanctuary (branch map 698), open to a PC with high Hollow Standing.
  • At Rowan's tribunal in Lucent, Sable is exposed as Sabine Kroll. With the Ironreach evidence (key IRONREACH_EVIDENCE) the exposure is public and immediate. Without it, only the PC and Ilsa learn the truth, and the public believes it later and less.
  • The Fault hits and Rowan escapes.
  • Act IV: the Lantern Hollow. Rowan reforms the movement as the Lantern Hollow: no masks, no Splitters, and one goal, to carry the Plates home. They become uneasy allies who shelter refugees in Cragholm, guide the PC through Gravenmoor's barrows and fight beside Nadir during the Sump Purge. A die-hard Hammer remnant under a freed or escaped Kroll stays loyal to Meridian's money.
  • Act V: in the crowd. Rowan, Wick, Thistle and (if alive) Teo come to the Festival disguised as Choir porters. During the hijack they guard the Concordance Ring pylons. The Hollow Spring grey ending is the dark version of everything they wanted, reached if the PC follows their original creed all the way.

Hollow Standing: what it unlocks (story side)

  • High: the Hollow Sanctuary branch; Wick teaches the Hymn's missing verse early; Bereaved grunts stand down instead of fighting (fewer grunt battles in Nadir); Thistle's defection in Ch14 happens earlier and warmer.
  • Low: Hollow cells attack the PC as "Meridian's pet" in Act III, and the Hollow Spring ending is locked.

"Light for everyone." Velmora's energy company. It harvests Imprints with Lattices, stores the power in Prism Cells, sells it to every kingdom (and, under a quarantine exemption, abroad), and since Act II it polices the realm under the Mandate. It is the nicest-looking villain in the game: clean uniforms, real jobs, real light.

The Arbiter Engine (Voss's plan, in plain terms)

  1. Eighteen Plates in convergence. The Festival's opening ceremony provides them.
  2. A keystone. The Legend Plate: the Crown's shard plus the PC's splinter. The keystone cradle is salvaged from drowned Aurelia in Ch17.
  3. Calibration. Resonance data from Plate-attuned battles. Every badge the PC earns is recorded by the Meridian badge case Voss gave them on the ferry. Every Festival match on the Prism Arena is a calibration run.
  4. Power. Originally the Dark Stone (Zekrom) in a Cogsworth core. After the PC frees Zekrom in Ch16, Voss reroutes to Lumen's whole grid fed into the arena, which is why Rook cutting the grid in the finale matters.
  5. The result. An artificial Judgment that pulls Arceus down into a new Gilded Chain, made permanent. Voss calls it "the last utility".

Weak points (each tied to a player choice; triggers in 10_CHOICES_ENDINGS): - Telemetry sabotage (Ch14): false calibration data. - Zekrom freed (Ch16): the original core is lost. - Magnus's governor (Ch16/21). - Rook's grid cut (Ch21). - Nyx's comms blackout (Ch21). - Calla's schematics (Ch20).

The more of these the PC has in place, the more the Voss and Arceus fights tilt toward Harmony, and the more ending options open. If none are in place, the Gilded Chain Bad ending is live.

How Meridian changes across the acts

  • Act I: patron. Voss is kind on the ferry. The Fellowship card pays for the PC's Potions. Linesmen give directions. Meridian sponsors the gyms (Rook is literally on its billboards). At the Gala, after the Shatter, Meridian restores the light and Voss is the hero. The Accord grants the Mandate. The darkness is visible only in the margins: Tamsin's contract, the stuttering Ebb, Larch's frown.
  • Act II: protector. Mandate checkpoints go up, first in Nadir. Directorate officers "escort" the PC "for their safety". Quent turns up at Cindervault running the geothermal tap that nearly blows the caldera, and his battle there is the PC's first Meridian admin fight. After Ironreach, Meridian takes Rowan into its own custody, not the Accord's. That is the first moment players should feel a chill.
  • Act III: the velvet fist.
  • Mandate curfews in Skyreach. The Larch Papers are answered with a smear campaign, then a frame-up (the Skyreach relay bombing).
  • The Gale Post raid, where the PC battles Marchetti.
  • Voss's public charm stays perfect. When Kroll is exposed at Lucent, he publicly disowns her as a "rogue contractor", announces an inquiry, and gains in the polls.
  • Meridian test-fires the Engine in Cogsworth at the end of the act, which causes the Fault.
  • Act IV: exposed and unbothered.
  • The PC learns everything: the telemetry (Ch14), the Engine (Ch16) and the lineage (Ch17).
  • The Sump Purge (Ch16): after the Fault exposes Aurelia, Meridian's Directorate "secures" Underveil and Nadir's lower districts, blaming Sweetrot gangs and the Hollow. Dozens die, Wenlow among them. Topside watches it on the news as "Operation Clear Air".
  • Nadir declares independence (Ch17). Councillor Sallow starts working against Voss from inside the Council.
  • Act V: host. Meridian runs Festival security under a Council vote that Anja's swing vote can't stop. It sponsors the arena, the broadcast, even the confetti. At the coronation, Voss hijacks the ceremony.

Meridian Standing: what it unlocks (story side)

  • High: the Meridian Black Site branch, where the PC can obtain the Type: Null line. Voss's deal in Ch17 is offered warmly, and Concord Dawn is available. Directorate patrols wave the PC through. Danger: high Standing plus untouched telemetry is the road to the Bad ending.
  • Low: Directorate ambush battles in Act III–IV, and the Fellowship card is cancelled in Ch12, which the PC hears about from a Mart clerk.

THE ACCORD

"Eighteen in Concord, One in Keeping." The three-hundred-year-old treaty government: the Sovereign, the eighteen Wardens, the Chancellor's bureaucracy, the Wardguard and the Prism Choir. It is the realm's legitimacy and its oldest lie.

Structure

  • The Sovereign: Valen Corrigan, since AF 270. He holds the Crown and performs the Tuning.
  • The Accord Council: the Sovereign and eighteen Wardens, with Chancellor Ambrose Hollis as administrator. Hollis is quietly on Meridian's payroll and passes the Mandate in Act II.
  • The Wardguard: the Accord's soldiers, who guard Plates against kings. Since Kaito Ren left, they are mostly ceremonial, apart from their worst job: sealing Quiet Towns.
  • The Prism Choir: clergy who sing the Tuning liturgy, led by Hierophant Maud Ellery. The PC's mother was a cantor.
  • The Keepers: the hidden circle who know the secret articles. Valen, Brann ("the Sovereign's Hand"), the Underveil Wardens (Nyx's line), Gloaming's Warden (Celestine) and, unofficially, Ilsa, who read it out of her father's mind.
  • The Accord Archive (branch map 838) is open to a PC with high Accord Standing. It holds the Concordance's Secret Articles in Maren's handwriting.

The Accord's lies (in order of discovery)

  1. That the Bleed is new and manageable. It has been worsening for a century, and the Quiet Towns were removed from maps.
  2. That the Tuning is a symbolic lightshow. It is the only thing holding the Plates quiet, and it kills Sovereigns.
  3. That Morwen Vane caused the Glassfall. Valen scapegoated her to keep the Tuning secret.
  4. That the Prismfall was a gift. It was a god escaping a chain.
  5. That the Plates are Velmora's forever. The founders swore they were borrowed, and Odo Prell erased the promise in AF 140.

How the Accord changes across the acts

  • Act I: distant majesty. Festival posters, the counting rhyme, Valen's broadcast ("every ten years, Velmora endures"). Valen appears in person after the Lowlight Shatter. He grants the Mandate because he believes a Hollow at war with Meridian is a bigger threat than Meridian.
  • Act II: the Conclave. The Accord convenes in Ironreach to vote on extending the Mandate. The Hollow attack hands Hollis the votes. Valen sees the PC in the Bleed burst and recognizes what they are.
  • Act III: fault lines.
  • The Council splits into blocs (see 01_WORLD_LORE §9).
  • The kingdoms test the Accord: Elowen claims the Meadow Plate, Isenmar declares martial law, the Dominar threatens war.
  • Valen backs Juno's succession and personally presides at Rowan's tribunal, where Kaito's Marrowgate testimony cracks the Accord's moral authority if the PC encouraged it.
  • Act IV: the lie exposed. Bastian, the Gravenmoor echo and Brann reveal the truth about the Fracture and the Tuning. Valen does not deny it. He says only: "Win your last two badges. Then come to me, and I'll tell you everything."
  • Act V: confession and sacrifice. Valen confesses (Ch20), fights the PC in the Final, and dies holding the Concordance Ring during the Arceus battle. What becomes of the Accord afterward depends on the ending: dissolved, reformed into a council of equals, or continued under a new Crown.

Accord Standing: what it unlocks (story side)

  • High: the Accord Archive branch, early access to Brann's confession in Act IV, and Valen's audience in Ch20 is longer and gentler. Sigrun's trial is shorter.
  • Low: the Wardguard won't escort the PC to Crownhaven, so the PC must take the smugglers' route over the Glassfall Scar. Valen's audience is colder. The Borrowed Crown ending is harder to reach.

THE PROTAGONIST (PC)

  • Name/gender: player-chosen. Age 15. Born in Lower Crownhaven, AF 285. Raised abroad in a small fishing town across the Hallow Strait (deliberately left unnamed).
  • Status: Unimprinted. Meridian scanners read them as NULL. They can enter Bleed zones unharmed, and Bleed-maddened Pokémon calm down near them. Secret: a splinter of the Legend Plate is lodged beneath their sternum. They have a small, star-shaped, colorless scar there that they've had "since forever".
  • Mother: Ines, a former cantor in the Prism Choir. She fled Velmora with the five-year-old PC on the night ferry after the AF 290 Glassfall and wilted for ten years in exile (see 01_WORLD_LORE, "Wilting"). She died last winter. Her final letter, which the player can reread from the Key Items pocket, reads:

    "Go to Imelda Larch at Ferrow's Landing, before they close the strait. Don't let anyone scan you. Don't go to Crownhaven. I'm sorry I never told you why. I wanted one of us to have an ordinary life. — Mum." The PC is scanned within five minutes of landing. They go to Crownhaven anyway.

  • Voice and choices: The PC is mostly silent, but every major scene offers 2–3 choices, usually a kind option, a blunt option and a silent option. Silence is a real choice that some characters read as strength and others as coldness. Choices move relationship variables (see FLAGS.md). The PC speaks full, authored lines in four places: 1. Ch13, the Mindscape: their five-year-old self speaks, and the PC answers. 2. Ch15, after K6: the player picks one of three things to say to Kestrel, and each one is a complete authored line. 3. Ch20, Valen's audience: the PC answers Valen's confession. 4. Ch21, before Arceus: the final choice is spoken aloud.
  • Arc: The PC goes from object to agent. Every faction treats them as a key, a weapon, a vessel or a vote. The arc completes when they stop being carried and choose what they carry.

THE THREE POWERS

Aurelian Voss — CEO of Meridian Concord (MERIDIAN_VOSS; final battle ~lv 98 on :PRISM_ARENA)

  • Age 47. Tall and silver at the temples. Bespoke charcoal suit with a single gold pin of Meridian's sunburst. He never raises his voice. Warm handshake, perfect memory for names. He drinks tea from a chipped company mug he's had since he was a linesman.

Why champions of other worlds are in Velmora (in-universe)

The Festival of Champions has always been a festival of other worlds. When the Prismfall tore Velmora, it opened a seam behind the forests of Elowen: Antumbra, the Kingdom Behind the Glass, Giratina's domain. The Glass is a mirror that faces outward as well as inward. Since AF 140, the Accord has sent its invitations through Antumbra's Veil Gate, and they reach the champions of worlds that already brush against each other: Reborn, Aevium and Ayrith.

These worlds already leak into one another, so the cameos aren't surprised: - ✔ Desolation's Champion Shiv turns up in Reborn's post-game. - ✔ Reborn's Adrienn and Aya help rebuild Gearen City in Aevium. - ✔ Aevium's Flora appears in Reborn.

To them, Velmora is just a new door. That is also why the quarantine doesn't stop them: they don't cross the Hallow Strait, they step through the Glass. The Veiled Synod opens the Gate for Festival week only. Giratina allows it, and Ch20 shows its eye in the Gate as each champion passes.

Rules of the Glass (story constraint, useful for balance): a traveler can bring only what is theirs. Anything bound to their home world's gods stays behind. That is why Lin arrives without her Arceus, and it is the single most important thing she says all week.
