# Trainer Field Playbook

How every important opponent uses their field (SPINE Revision 10, item 4). For each one: the field, the game plan, the
transition play, how they answer the obvious flip, and what the player is meant to do about it.

- Data: `pbs/trainers.txt`, generated by `tools/trainer_gen/gen.py` (+ `routes.py`, `cameos.py`). Edit the generator, not the PBS.
- Field rules: `design/FIELDS.md`. Level curve and difficulty tiers: `design/BALANCE.md`.
- Sets below are **Standard**. Hard adds a Pokémon and sharper sets; Casual drops one Pokémon and the nastiest tool
  (usually the seed, the weather or the transition move).
- Check any time: `ruby tools/trainer_gen/dump_fields.rb && python3 -I tools/trainer_gen/field_fit.py --all`
  (lists each team's field hooks and fails on a move or ability the field switches off).

"Transform strike" = the move that completes a field change hits 1.3× harder. A "stage play" changes a stage inside
the same field (no strike unless the field says so).

## 2. Early game (Chapters 0–2, Gyms 1–3)

**SPINE Revision 14 swapped Saltmere and Briarwick.** Gym 1 is Dorian (Saltmere, Normal, Grand Stage, cap 15, single);
Gym 2 is Tamsin (Briarwick, Bug, Silkwood, cap 19, double). Route order: Ferrow's Landing → coast → Saltmere → inland
→ Gossamer Wood / Briarwick → north → Rimewood / Frostwick → Ebbmouth. Trainer IDs did not change
(`[LEADER_DORIAN,Dorian,0]`, `[LEADER_TAMSIN,Tamsin,0]`); `Prismfall::GYMS` (Core) now has Dorian at index 1. Which
route map sits on which stretch is provisional until `design/SWAP_GYM1_GYM2.md` lands (one table, `routes.py` `PLAN`).

Design rule for this stretch: one new field idea per fight, the Gym Guide's advice must work, the Warden's counter to it
is one Pokémon the player can remove, and Standard is tuned against a realistic early team in the harness (§6).

### K1: Kestrel — no field (Tidewatch Station, Lv 5, can be lost)
The starter with the type edge, Lv 5, **no type move** on Standard (Scratch / Growl, Tackle / Tail Whip, Tackle /
Growl), as Reborn's Victoria and Rejuvenation's Ren open. With the type move it won 60 of 60 against realistic Lv 5
starters; without it the player wins about one in six, the upset the script has a line for. Hard keeps the type move
and adds Starly; Casual is Lv 3. (A non-Gen 1 pick gets its level-up moves from Core, so the type move may be back.)

### J1: Juno — Meadow (Saltmere promenade green, before Gym 1, cap 15)
Cottonee 13 *(−C)*, Petilil 13 *(−C)*, Bounsweet "Sweetpea" 13, starter 15 (unevolved). Hard adds Fidough.
- **Plan.** Fairy Wind and Razor Leaf are ×1.5 in the Meadow breeze; Leaf Guard is always on; Petilil's Growth and
  Aromatherapy keep the Grass side going.
- **Transition play.** A Prankster **Grassy Terrain** turns the Meadow into a Flower Garden at once, the field she
  will defend at Gym 9; the terrain lasts 8 turns there.
- **Counterplay.** Two Fire attacks burn a Meadow (one in sun); the friendliest place to learn that.

### Gym 1: Dorian — Grand Stage, single battle (cap 15)
Reuben's locked troupe (2026-11-23): Smeargle 9, Chatot 11, Hisuian Zorua 11, Ditto 11 *(−C)*, Kecleon 12 *(−C)*,
**Meloetta 14** (ace); skill 100 (the ace waits for last). Hard: Sash Smeargle (Parting Shot), Sing Chatot 13, Hisuian
Zorua 12 (Synthetic Seed, Shadow Sneak), Imposter Ditto, Protean Kecleon 13, Meloetta IV 12. Casual: Smeargle 7,
Chatot 10, Zorua 10, Meloetta 13.
Reference: Reborn's Julia and Rejuvenation's Venam bring six at cap −3 .. cap with berries, a Gem and field plays.
- **The show builds.** Smeargle's Fake Out and Pay Day are showpieces (×1.5); Relic Song is a damaging sound move.
  Two of a kind raise the applause; any crit does too. At Standing Ovation Meloetta's **Sing never misses**, every
  attack gets +1 crit stage and Normal types bask (1/16).
- **No Chatter, no Echoed Voice, no Round on Standard.** In the harness a sound-move Chatot on the Grand Stage
  (Normal ×1.3 and sound ×1.3–1.5 on STAB) swept realistic teams: Echoed Voice 8%, Round 13%. Standard Chatot is Peck /
  Confide / Encore / Growl, a nuisance, not a sweeper; Hard has Sing (its Lv 13 move) and no damaging sound move.
- **Ace: the Act One Matinee.** Meloetta (Serene Grace, Hardy, IV 0 / EV 0, no held item) is the 1-bar
  `:DORIAN_MELOETTA_MATINEE` (Bosses 111): every stat ×0.80 (Hard ×0.85, Casual ×0.78), no pips, no status immunity.
  Relic Song swaps Aria ↔ Pirouette and takes the Normal ×1.3 only: Grand Stage's sound boost skips it
  (`102_Field_GrandStage.rb` `:not_moves => [:RELICSONG]`). Hisuian Zorua's Illusion wears Meloetta's face.
  Full power is the post-game Crownhaven Encore (`PG_MELOETTA`) and the Festival Pageant Bout (v90: Lv 90, Throat Spray).
- **Counterplay.** Rock and Ghost (Nacli, Dwebble, Sandygast), stalling moves to calm the crowd, KO the singers first.
  No Fighting type exists before Gym 1 (Rev 16 distribution).
- **The Saltmere audience (SPINE Rev 19).** The townsfolk met set the opening stage (0–6: 1, 7–13: 2, 14+: 3), and
  from a good house up your fans heal your side 1/16 a round. At Standing Ovation a good house lets his troupe bask
  at half; a full house makes it yours: only your side basks, only your attacks get the crit stage, and Jigglypuff's
  Sing can miss (done 2026-11-20). Standard (realistic six, 180 runs each): sparse 45%, good 63%, full 73%; Lv 14 team
  22 / 32 / 45%; Hard prepared 33 / 50 / 62%. Before the rules: 43 / 52 / 45%.

### Pallbearer Thistle — Silkwood (Briarwick, before Gym 2, cap 19)
Dustox 16, Foongus 17 (not on Casual), Phantump "Lowmarsh" 18. Hard adds Whirlipede.
- **Theme.** A weaver's Bug/Poison team (02_FACTIONS) plus the glassed Phantump named for her Quiet Town.
- **Plan.** Dustox glides on the silk (Bug-type fliers: Flying ×1.2) and Shield Dust ignores the player's add-ons;
  powders are ×1.2 accurate; Leech Seed and Mega Drain outlast a team slowed by fresh silk.
- **Transition.** She doesn't burn her own trade's wood, but if the player does, Embers favour Grass and Ghost and
  heal her Grass types; two growing moves turn the ash into a Meadow.
- **Version.** The Ch0–2 script now says version 0, matching the PBS (named admins count rematches from 0).

### Gym 2: Tamsin — Silkwood, double battle (cap 19)
Joltik 14 + Surskit 15 lead, Dwebble 16 *(−C)*, Swadloon "Bobbin" 19; skill 100. Hard adds Vivillon (Rage Powder) and
sharper items (Focus Sash, Eviolite, Silver Powder, Damp Rock).
- **Theme: "a thread holds because it's twisted with others."** Every Pokémon makes its partner better.
- **Lead pair.** Surskit's **Rain Dance** wets the silk (every Electric move ×1.2) for Joltik's **Electroweb**, which
  in a double hits both foes (×1.5, never misses, Speed −1, 30% paralysis); rain also doubles Surskit's Speed.
- **The bird answer (Rev 16 study, point 2).** Birds are common before Gym 2 and Flying is the obvious answer to Bug,
  so **Dwebble** replaced Spidops: Smack Down grounds a bird into the silk (non-Bug fliers are ×0.8 there anyway) and
  Rock Tomb slows both answers; Electroweb hits them for 2×. Spidops held 51 of 52 harness losses.
- **Back line.** Bobbin holds the **Telluric Seed** (+1 Def, +1 Sp. Def, −1 Speed) and Protect; its Razor Leaf hits both.
- **Answer to the flip.** Three Fire attacks set the wood ablaze; the rain halves Fire and douses a Wildfire to Embers.
- **Counterplay.** Fire and Rock (before her: no Fire / Rock / Flying gift; wild Fire and Rock ≤ 4%), spread moves,
  knock Surskit out before it rains.

### Teo — Water Surface (Ebbmouth, before Gym 3)
Carvanha 19 (not on Casual), Arrokuda 20, Chewtle "Snapper" 21. Hard adds Basculin.
- **Plan.** A Swift Swim rush (Arrokuda; Snapper has Swift Swim too) with Water ×1.5. No transition play on purpose:
  this fight introduces the field one battle before the gym teaches its changes.

### K2: Kestrel — Mountain (Gullcry Cliffs, cap 23)
Staravia 21 *(−C)*, Sandile 21 *(−C)*, Honedge "Morwen" 21, starter 23. Hard adds Deino; Hard Morwen dropped Swords Dance
for Fury Cutter (Swords Dance + Shadow Sneak swept prepared teams).
- Flying ×1.2; Staravia's **Telluric Seed** gives +1 Speed and a Tailwind for his side (6 turns here). Rock is ×1.5
  on the cliffs (Sandile's Rock Tomb) and Moxie snowballs. Stall the Tailwind out with Protect.

### Gym 3: Marisol — Water Surface (cap 23)
Pelipper 19, Chinchou 20 *(−C)*, Buizel 20, Gyarados "Lucky" 23. Hard: Lanturn for Chinchou, + Dewpider, Floatzel for
Buizel, Tailwind Pelipper, Ice Fang Lucky.
- **Plan.** Water ×1.5, Swift Swim without rain, and the player's grounded non-Water Pokémon wade at ×0.75 Speed.
  Lucky's **Whirlpool** is ×2 here and traps.
- **No Dive (round 3).** The Water Surface ↔ Underwater loop strikes ×1.3 both ways (Dive in, Dive out): it was 103
  knockouts in 60 harness runs, so neither Standard nor Hard Lucky has Dive. **Loop fixed (field owner, 2026-11-20):**
  Dive now moves the battle on the turn it strikes, diving down is the only transform strike and surfacing is not
  (`:strike_turn` / `:boost => false`), so one Dive is one change. Lucky can have Dive back if wanted.
- **Answer to the flip.** Chinchou's Volt Absorb takes the Electric answer and its Spark is ×1.5 against grounded
  targets; Pelipper's Flying moves cover Grass.
- **Counterplay.** Electric from a Pokémon that isn't grounded, or Grass; hazards and Ground moves fail on the water.
- **The Fire starter's way in (2026-11-20):** Emolga, 5% at night in the secret Rimewood Glade (563): airborne, so
  the surface doesn't slow it, and faster than everything but Swift Swim Buizel. A Fire-starter six with it wins 42% on
  Standard (without it 0–3%); BALANCE.md §9.
- Standard tuning: Buizel for Floatzel (Ice Fang Floatzel was a top killer), Modest Lucky, Oran Berries.

## 3. Gyms 4–18

### Gym 4: Rook — Neon Grid (27)
Luxio, Electabuzz, Toxtricity, Rotom-Wash "Camera".
- **Plan.** Electric ×1.5. Toxtricity's Eerie Impulse drops Sp. Atk three stages here; Camera's Hydro Pump carries
  the grid's 30% paralysis, which feeds its Hex.
- **Seed.** Electabuzz: **Synthetic Seed** (+1 Sp. Atk and Magnet Rise).
- **Stage play / answer to the flip.** The player blacks the grid out with two quakes or two floods. Charge (Luxio,
  Electabuzz, Toxtricity) reboots it in two uses and gives +1 Sp. Atk here; Luxio's Bite is his hedge while it is
  dark (Dark ×1.3). Electabuzz floats over the quakes and Camera levitates.
- **Counterplay.** Ground types, then keep the grid dark: Electric damage swings between ×0.5 and ×2 in a fixed
  cycle that can be read. Camera's own Hydro Pump counts toward a Blackout.

### Gym 5: Sahar — Desert (31)
Hippopotas, Sandaconda, Krokorok, Claydol "Effigy".
- **Plan.** Sand Tomb ×2, Dig ×1.5, Ground types take ×0.67 from special attacks, sandstorm lasts 8 + 3 turns.
- **Seed.** Sandaconda: **Telluric Seed**.
- **Stage play / answer to the flip.** Rain or three floods wake a Superbloom (Grass ×1.5). Sand Stream, Sand Spit
  (it triggers when the Water move lands) and two of Krokorok's Fire Fangs each bake it back to dunes.
- **Counterplay.** Grass, with rain timed after Hippopotas and Sandaconda are down. Water is ×0.8 here.
- She makes no cross-field change herself. A player's two searing moves (Overheat, Eruption...) fuse her dunes into the
  **Glass Dunes** (Fire ×1.2, no sand, no Dig), and her quakes crack the glass back into her Desert in three (her
  `[:DESERT, 1] => 22` want rewards that; each crack also leaves broken glass on the player's side, and the player's
  Fire anneals a crack; Effigy's Rapid Spin sweeps the broken glass off her side); the sea-storm (heavy rain) is the
  only way to the flood. Left four rounds without Fire the glass cools into Mirror Glass (Rev 24).

### Gym 6: Corvin — Corrosive Mire, double battle (35)
- **Doubles (Round 2).** Lead Weezing + Amoonguss. Weezing's Heat Wave hits both foes (and each use scorches grounded
  non-Fire foes 1/8 on the mire); Protect on the frail pair; Crobat's Tailwind; Ward's Baneful Bunker. Hard: Rage Powder
  Amoonguss redirects, Fake Out Salazzle, Wide Guard Ward (Amoonguss only learns Rage Powder at 40).
Galarian Weezing, Crobat, Amoonguss, Drapion, Toxapex "Ward".
- **Plan.** Poison ×1.5 and it hits Steel; Venoshock is always doubled against grounded targets; Poison types
  fling poison each turn and heal 1/16. Ward's Merciless gets +1 Attack on entry and crits whatever is poisoned.
- **Seed.** Crobat: **Synthetic Seed** (+1 Atk, +1 Sp. Atk, holder poisoned; a Poison type cannot be poisoned).
- **Transition play.** Weezing's **Heat Wave** ignites the marsh gas (1/8 to the player's grounded non-Fire Pokémon
  per use) and the second one burns it off to a Wasteland with a transform strike. Quakes are halved there, which
  blunts the Ground answer; his team keeps Poison ×1.2 and Toxic Boost.
- **Answer to the flip.** The player's Fire does the same thing to him; Ward's Scald answers Fire types. Weezing
  never has Misty Surge (Misty Terrain purifies the mire).
- **Counterplay.** Ground and Psychic before the Wasteland; Misty Terrain or two Purifies to wash the field away.

### Gym 7: Brann — Volcanic (39)
Torkoal, Camerupt, Magmortar, Arcanine, Coalossal "Anvil".
- **Plan.** Lava Plume and Earth Power prime an eruption at the end of the round: his Fire types get +1, lava
  rains on the player's grounded Pokémon, hazards are blown away. Drought halves Water.
- **Seed.** Camerupt: **Elemental Seed** (+1 Atk, Sp. Atk and Speed; the burn cannot touch a Fire type). It also
  has Magma Armor now (+1 Def on entry, 1/16 a turn, +1 Def/Sp. Def per eruption).
- **Transition play.** After a quench, three Lava Plumes (Torkoal, Camerupt) re-melt Cinderglass to Volcanic.
- **Answer to the flip.** The quench is the intended answer, so Anvil is built for Cinderglass: Steam Engine gains
  Speed every turn there and Rock Slide is ×1.3 on obsidian.
- **Counterplay.** Rain Dance is the best single move in this fight: it replaces the sun and quenches the field at
  once. Otherwise two Surfs, with Torkoal removed first.
- Removed: Stealth Rock on Torkoal and Coalossal (his own eruptions blew it away) and Yawn (eruptions wake sleepers).

### Gym 8: Kaito — Colosseum (43)
Hawlucha, Machamp, Hitmontop, Conkeldurr, Lucario "Memory".
- **Plan.** Punches and kicks ×1.2 on top of Fighting ×1.2; every knockout gives +1 Attack; two knockouts start a
  Frenzy (+1 crit stage for everything).
- **Seed.** Hawlucha: **Synthetic Seed** (+1 Attack, Focus Energy, Taunted). Eating it powers Acrobatics up; the set is
  four attacks. On Hard it also triggers Unburden; Standard Hawlucha is Limber since round 4 (§8b).
- **Abilities.** Machamp's No Guard is +1 Attack on entry; Memory's Inner Focus is +1 crit stage, with a Scope Lens.
- **Stage play.** The Frenzy comes from knockouts. Nobody on the team uses a quake (three would drop the pit into a Cave).
- **Counterplay.** Two stalling moves calm a Frenzy. Psychic (special Psychic is booed, ×0.8), Flying, Fairy,
  Intimidate; burns except on Conkeldurr.

### Gym 9: Juno (J3) — Flower Garden (47)
Whimsicott, Shiinotic, Lilligant, starter, Tsareena "Sweetpea".
- **Plan.** Grow the garden: Whimsicott's **Telluric Seed** (+1 stage on entry, Ingrain, +1 Sp. Def), Prankster
  Growth and Leech Seed, Lilligant's Aromatherapy, and every second Grass attack. Petal Dance is a petal move and
  Own Tempo never tires of it.
- **Answer to the flip.** Fire prunes the garden a stage per hit; her answer is regrowth speed. At stage 5 the
  overgrowth makes Grass weaknesses neutral, but two Fire attacks then set it ablaze. If that happens, Growth and
  Leech Seed turn the Embers back into a Meadow.
- **Counterplay.** Hold Fire until stage 5 and burn it down, or cut it back early with Fire and blades. Acid moves
  reset it to stage 1. The harness challenger won this way in 8 turns: the garden is strong and flammable by design.

### Gym 10: Anja — Frozen Lake (51)
Alolan Ninetales, Cloyster, Beartic, Walrein, Avalugg, Mamoswine "Matriarch".
- **Plan.** Ice ×1.5, Slush Rush without hail, Aurora Veil without hail, priority and Ice Shard slide for +1 Speed.
- **Seed.** Matriarch: **Elemental Seed** (+2 Speed for 1/8 HP).
- **Transition play.** Avalugg's **Body Press** and Matriarch's **High Horsepower** are heavy blows; three crack the
  lake to open water with a transform strike. That halves the challenger's Fire moves and feeds Walrein's Surf and
  Cloyster's Razor Shell.
- **Answer to the flip.** Melting the lake is what a Fire team does anyway, so the destination is covered above.
  Each of her Ice attacks refreezes one crack, and Blizzard / Freeze-Dry refreeze open water in three.
- **Counterplay.** Fighting and Steel rather than Fire; Electric and Grass once it is water.
- Matriarch uses High Horsepower instead of Earthquake: quakes lay Spikes on both sides here and fail on open water.
- **Rev 24 field audit:** sunlight no longer melts the lake at once. Three rounds of sun thaw it (each of her Ice
  attacks sets the thaw back a round, and Ninetales's Snow Warning ends the sun), so a Drought or Sunny Day player
  has to hold the sky for three rounds; only Desolate Land melts it at once. Win rates: `BALANCE.md` §9c.

### Gym 11: Odessa — Stormfront, double battle (55)
- **Doubles (Round 2).** Lead Pelipper + Noivern: Pelipper's Tailwind (6 turns here) and **Wide Guard** against the
  Rock Slides and Discharges a flock fears; Noivern's Hyper Voice and Swanna's Icy Wind (wind, ×1.2) hit both foes;
  Oricorio's Helping Hand; Kilowattrel and Pelipper carry Protect.
Pelipper, Swanna, Pom-Pom Oricorio, Noivern, Kilowattrel, Corviknight "Taxi".
- **Plan.** Hurricane and Thunder never miss; Tailwind lasts two turns longer. Kilowattrel's Wind Power arrives
  charged; Swanna's Big Pecks is +1 Defense on entry.
- **Seed.** Oricorio: **Elemental Seed** (+1 Speed and Charge, so the first Electric Revelation Dance is doubled).
- **Transition play.** Noivern's **Sunny Day** twice tears the storm open into a Rainbow: added effects are doubled
  (Air Slash flinches 60%, Hurricane confuses 60%) and Normal is ×1.5 for Hyper Voice. No strike: it is a status move.
- **Answer to the flip.** Electric and Rock are the answers. Kilowattrel replaces Talonflame as the Electric check;
  Taxi's Iron Head handles Rock and Ice.
- **Counterplay.** Electric is ×1.3 here for the player too; Smack Down is ×2. Defog brings the storm back.
- No Drizzle any more: in rain the Stormfront's lightning strikes every airborne non-Electric Pokémon, which was her
  own team.

### Gym 12: Ilsa — Mindscape (59)
Bronzong, Musharna, Espeon, Gothitelle, Reuniclus, Hatterene "Hush".
- **Plan.** Trick Room (it lasts 7 turns here), Psychic ×1.5.
- **Transition play.** Hush's **Psychic Noise** is a damaging sound move: the third one cracks the dream into the
  Inverse Field with a transform strike. There the Dark, Ghost and Bug answers are resisted, Psychic hits Dark
  types for double, and Trick Room lasts 8 turns. Lucent sits in the Reverse Kingdom; this is home.
- **Answer to the flip.** Three Dark attacks curdle the dream into a Nightmare (Dark ×1.5). Two Calm Minds
  (Musharna, Espeon, Gothitelle) soothe it back; Reuniclus's Focus Blast is for the Dark types.
- **Counterplay.** Dark and Ghost early, Taunt on the Trick Room setters, and Topsy-Turvy rights an inverted field.
- Psychic Noise is not in the base learnset data (it is a Scarlet/Violet TM); it is on the validator allowlist.

### Gym 13: Bastian — Crystal Cavern (63)
Carbink, Alolan Golem, Aurorus, Dusk Lycanroc, Rhyperior, Gigalith "Geode".
- **Plan.** Light moves ×1.5 (Power Gem, Dazzling Gleam, Flash Cannon, Ancient Power), Rock ×1.3, screens last two
  turns longer.

## 8. AI support for the plays

`plugins/Prismfall Field Effects/012_FieldEffects_TrainerPlays.rb` (new; alias-chained, no core file edited):
- `WANTS`: per trainer type, which field changes its team is built for (and which to avoid). It adds a score to a move
  that advances or completes that change, counts weather / terrain moves whose weather triggers a transition (Rain
  Dance on a Volcanic field, Misty Terrain on a mire), and stops the AI from treating an `:even_if_failed` transition
  move as a failed move (Noivern's Sunny Day on the Stormfront).
- `ENTRY_FIELDS`: the field follows a trainer's Pokémon as it enters (the Eevee Octave finale).
- `SUPPORT`: in doubles, a bonus for support moves the v21 AI underrates against raw damage (Tailwind, Wide Guard,
  Helping Hand, Follow Me, Fake Out and Decorate on the first turns, Rain Dance when it isn't raining).
- Found and fixed in round 2: `Battle::AI::AITrainer` has no public reader for its trainer, so the first version of
  these hooks never fired (round-1 "plays used" numbers are base-AI behaviour). They look the trainer up through
  `battle.pbGetOwnerFromBattlerIndex` now; a debug-log run confirms the bonus is applied.
- `plugins/Prismfall Bosses/105_Boss_Mourner.rb` (new): Trevenant "Glasswood" is a 2-bar boss (3 on Hard) through a
  trainer-boss rule; each break cures it, resets drops, +1 Def and roots it (Ingrain).
