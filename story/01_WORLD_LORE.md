# Story Bible 01: World Lore — Velmora

> Geography follows `design/WORLD.md` §1 (World lead). Kingdom names are final per SPINE Revision 3. Calendar: **AF** = After the Fall.

---

## 1. Cosmology in one breath

**Arceus, the Original One,** shaped the world and keeps it in balance through its **eighteen Plates**, one for each type. At its heart sits a nineteenth, the **Legend Plate**, which holds *every type and none*. The Plates are not tools Arceus carries. They are Arceus, the way a heartbeat is part of a body. Separate them and Arceus becomes incomplete: it sleeps, wounded and unable to act, somewhere beyond the sky that Velmoran priests call the Hall of First Light.

For three hundred years Velmora has lived on pieces of a sleeping god.

## 2. History

### Before the Fall: the Aureate Kingdom
Before AF 0, one kingdom ruled the continent: **the Aureate Kingdom of House Aurel**. Its capital, **Aurelia**, rose in gold terraces up the southern sea-cliffs. The Aurels were builders and believers. Their court artificers, **the Gilders**, combined clockwork, alchemy and old Pokémon pacts into something close to science.

The kingdom's last two decades were **the Blight Years**: crop-rot, a wasting plague and three failed harvests running. King **Theodric Aurel**, later called **the Gilded King**, tried to fix the world by force.
- **First he tried to remake the land.** The Gilders woke Groudon beneath the Bone Sands and Kyogre off the western coast. The two titans nearly tore the continent in half before they were driven back to sleep. Ossaria's Unown records call this "the Year of Two Weathers". They met in Ossaria's western dunes, and the Dry Tide west of Duneholt is the seabed Kyogre's sea left there.
- **Then he went higher.** The Gilders forged **the Gilded Chain**: eighteen golden locks, one for each Plate, around a **keystone cradle** shaped for the Legend Plate. They meant to summon Arceus and bind it to the throne. *"If heaven will not kneel, we'll build a chain."* — Theodric, as quoted in "The Gilded King".

Theodric was not a monster. His people were starving, and he loved them. That is the point, and it is why Voss can quote him without irony.

### AF 0: the Prismfall
At midsummer, in the Sun Court of Aurelia, the Gilders performed the Calling. Arceus descended, and the Chain closed. For one heartbeat the god was bound.

Then Arceus did the only thing a god can do that a chain cannot hold: **it broke itself.**
- **The Plates.** It shed all eighteen Plates in a burst of light seen from every corner of the continent. They scattered across the land and buried themselves where they struck. Every Plate began to **Imprint** the ground around it with its type.
- **The Legend Plate,** caught in the keystone cradle, **shattered**.
- **Aurelia.** The shockwave collapsed the city into the cliffs. The sea poured into the ruins, and Aurelia became **the Drowned Crown**.
- **Isenmar.** The surge rolled west and north up the coast and woke Kyogre. At that moment the **Icicle Plate** fell into the waves and froze Kyogre and the sea around it solid. That frozen sea is Isenmar.
- **Antumbra.** Behind the great forest, the world *tore*. A mirrored reverse side opened behind the Glass. Giratina, the guardian of reverse places, came through to hold the seam closed.
- **Arceus,** incomplete, fled beyond the sky and slept.

### AF 0–4: the Grey Years
Survivors gathered around the fallen Plates because the Plates kept them alive. Fire that never went out. Water that never froze. Fields that bloomed in a week. Cities grew around each **Imprint**. Families who kept the Plates safe from raiders became the first **Wardens**.

### AF 4: the Concordance
Theodric's daughter **Princess Maren Aurel** had opposed the Calling and survived the Fall. She pulled the largest surviving shard of the Legend Plate from the wreck of the keystone cradle. She renounced her house, took the name **Maren Ilse**, and climbed the high caldera at the center of the continent, where all eighteen Imprints can be seen glowing on a clear night.

There she gathered the eighteen Plate-keeping families and the war-chiefs of the provinces that would become the kingdoms. Together they signed **the Concordance**, founding **the Accord**. The caldera city became **Crownhaven**.

**Public articles** (taught in every schoolroom):
1. Every Plate is held *in trust for all Velmora* by its Warden. No king may own or seize a Plate.
2. Wardens answer to the Accord, not to the crown of the land they stand on.
3. Once each decade, the Plates are carried to Crownhaven for the Festival, and the strongest trainer in the realm holds the Sovereign Seat.
4. Motto: ***"Eighteen in Concord, One in Keeping."*** (Everyone thinks "One" means the Sovereign. It means the Legend Plate.)

**Secret articles** (the Sovereign, the Hierophant, the Underveil Keepers and Gloaming's Warden alone):
- **The Tuning.** Left alone, the Plates *reach for each other*. This is the Bleed. Maren set her Legend shard into **the Sovereign's Crown**. Every ten years, with all eighteen Plates gathered, the Crown-bearer hushes them for another decade.
- **The Hymn of Return.** The founders swore the Plates were *borrowed*. "When the land is healed, one of no color shall walk the eighteen roads, and carry them home." Keeping the Plates was meant to be **temporary**.

## 3. How Imprints work

**In-world science**, per Larch's *Field Notes* (Rev. 3 adds an in-game Field Notes encyclopedia, and these are her entries' voice):
- **The Imprint.** A Plate saturates the land around it with its type, typically within 5–15 km and strongest at the heart. Matter, weather and Pokémon behavior bend toward that type. This is not metaphor. **Field effects are Imprints**: fire really does burn hotter in Cindervault, sound really does carry on Saltmere's Grand Stage, and the ice really does drink heat in Rimeholt. Battles fought in an Imprint obey it.
- **Resources.** Each Imprint is an economy: Cindervault's ever-fire, the Ebb's two-way current, Verdance's endless harvest, Briarwick's silk that never tangles, Lumen City's free current, and so on.
- **Tint.** People and Pokémon raised in an Imprint carry a faint affinity for its type. Velmorans call it their **tint**, as in "she's Cindervault-tinted, she'll run warm." Tint shows on Meridian scanners as a color bar. Foreigners take on a tint after a few weeks. **The PC never does.**
- **Eevee** evolves according to the Imprint it is raised in. Velmorans call it "the Plate's child", and an Eevee is a traditional gift to a newborn. (This is the lore behind the Eevee Octave.)
- **Seeds.** Imprints condense into seeds in the right soil. **Elemental, Magical and Telluric Seeds** grow wild at Imprint borders and are harvested by seed-pickers. **Synthetic Seeds** are Meridian's lab-grown version, sold in every Mart since AF 294.
- **Terrain.** Electric, Grassy, Misty and Psychic Terrain are "small, temporary Imprints a Pokémon makes itself". Where they meet a real Imprint they layer over it, are smothered by it, or transform it. Locals know which, and NPCs should say so.

## 4. The Bleed

- **What it is:** the Plates reaching for each other. Each Imprint is slowly extending toward the others, and where two or more overlap, reality loses its grip on what type it is. In battle, that is the `:BLEED` field: unstable, with effects borrowed at random from other fields. It has accelerated sharply since AF 289, because **Meridian's Lattices draw on Imprints like a pump draws on a well**, and a drained Imprint reaches harder.
- **Bleed zones** look *beautiful* at first: glass orchards, forests of frozen birdsong, rivers hanging in the air. They are deadly to anyone with a tint. Every zone has a **Bleed Veil** at its edge, a shimmer in the air that tinted people physically cannot cross ("my skin's burning, I can't"). The PC walks through.
- **Fixing:** prolonged Bleed exposure Fixes a person into the substance of the overlapping Imprints. It goes in stages:
  1. Glassy fingernails.
  2. "Sleepwalking": staring, humming.
  3. Partial Fixing: crystal, ice, bark or salt spreads from the extremities.
  4. Full Fixing.

  The Fixed are not dead. They **dream**, and with time some stop dreaming. Corvin's Quiet Ward in Mirefen holds forty patients at stages 2–3. The Accord hides whole Fixed towns: the **Quiet Towns** (Marrowgate, Lowmarsh, Halden's Reach East).
- **Wilting:** a tinted person far from their Imprint slowly sickens with weakness, grey skin and grief. This is why Velmorans almost never emigrate, and why the outside world's quarantine is cruel twice over. The PC's mother wilted in exile for ten years to keep her child hidden.
- **The Shatter:** strike a Plate hard enough and it releases its Imprint all at once. The result is a Bleed explosion and instant Fixing within a few hundred meters. The Lumen Shatter (Act I) Fixed twelve people in the Lowlight district.

## 5. The Tuning and the Crown

- **The Sovereign's Crown** is a plain silver circlet holding a single colorless stone, **the largest surviving shard of the Legend Plate**.
- **The Tuning.** At the Festival's opening ceremony, the Wardens set their Plates into the eighteen pylons of the **Concordance Ring** in the arena. The Choir sings, and the Crown-bearer *hushes* the Plates' reaching. To the public it is a lightshow. In truth, the bearer's body is the conduit.
- **The cost.** The Crown's shard used to carry most of the load. It depletes a little every decade, and each Sovereign pays more than the last. Most Sovereigns since AF 140 have died within twenty years of their first Tuning, of what the Accord calls "the Sovereign's fatigue".
- **AF 290.** When Meridian's array cracked the Crown, a splinter flew free into the PC. Brann reset the stone, but the Crown has been incomplete ever since. Valen has carried nearly the whole Tuning in his body: his left arm is crystal to the shoulder, under the grey glove.
- **The ceremony is failing,** and Valen knows it. He cannot survive the AF 300 Tuning without help. With the PC's splinter rejoined, the Crown would be whole, and its bearer could hold the Plates quiet for a century, alone.

## 6. The Other Face of the Legend Plate (Resonance: Harmony ↔ Ruin)

The Legend Plate holds **every type, and none.** It has two faces, and the PC's choices tilt their shard toward one of them. This is the in-world meaning of the **Resonance** track (SPINE Rev. 3).
- **Harmony** (*every*): the shard calms. Bleed-maddened Pokémon settle near the PC, Bleed zones go quiet around them, and other Plates "hum along". This face makes Return possible.
- **Ruin** (*none*): the shard **unmakes**. Imprints thin to nothing where the PC stands, and a Plate touched in anger cracks. At its extreme it can unmake Judgment, the Glass, or the Plates themselves. Celestine's rhyme for it: *"the color that eats colors"*.
- **Story rules:**
  - Ruin is never "evil mode". It's the face that answers despair, the Hollow's grief in its purest form.
  - Wardens and Pokémon *feel* the PC's Resonance. Corvin: "You've gone cold, love." Celestine: "You're getting louder in the wrong key."
  - The **Ruin Route** (Narrative Systems spec) follows a PC who has seen every way the world ends (the Ledger) and decides to stop asking it to be saved.

## 7. Religion

- **The Prism Faith** (Church of the First Light). Velmora's established faith. Arceus is "the Original One". **The Benediction Doctrine** holds that the Prismfall was a *gift* of love, which is the Accord's official myth.
  - The **Prism Choir**, led by **Hierophant Maud Ellery**, performs the Tuning liturgy without knowing what it really does.
  - Churches have eighteen colored windows around a clear nineteenth pane. Prayers end with "and keep the One".
- **The Wound Doctrine** (heresy). The Plates are Arceus's wound, and Velmora is living off a god's blood. Taught by **Brother Wick** (defrocked) and radicalized by the Hollow into "pull the nails".
- **The Gloaming Liturgy.** Gloaming's dusk-chapels still sing the complete Hymn of Return in its original form. The Choir thinks it's a quaint local variant.
- **Antumbra's Veiled Faith.** Giratina is "the Warden of the Reverse", the god's own night watchman. The Veiled Synod's seers read reflections, and lantern-priests guide the dead.
- **Ashkaar's Fire-Creed.** The Dragon-Lords claim descent from the white dragon of truth (Reshiram). Oaths sworn over flame are binding, so lying in a Dominion court is a capital crime.
- **Isenmar's sea-songs** are about "the Sleeping Whale under the ice" (Kyogre). Fishermen pour a cup of warm broth through an ice-hole before every season.
- **Nadir's folk-saints:** "the Ogre in the Mask", a protector of the forgotten borrowed from Elowen's legend, painted on sump walls beside the Hollow's ring sigil.

## 8. Economy

- **The Imprint economy** is three centuries old. Each Warden city trades its Imprint's product, and the kingdoms tax the trade, though the Concordance forbids them from taxing the Plate itself. The kings call that "the Accord's theft".
- **Meridian Concord** changed everything in a decade:
  - **Lattices** are extraction pylons (Larch's design) that draw energy from an Imprint. Fourteen of eighteen cities have one by Act III.
  - **Prism Cells** are batteries charged from Lattices. Every lamp in Zenith runs on them. Under a special quarantine exemption, Meridian exports them to the Concert of Regions, so Velmora's suffering powers other people's cities.
  - **Royalties.** Meridian pays cities and crowns a cut, so Wardens and kings alike depend on it. Imprint industries that compete with Meridian (Ebbmouth's tidal mills, Briarwick's looms) are bought out or starved.
  - **The Mandate** (from Act II on): after the Lowlight Shatter, the Accord grants **Meridian Security** police powers wherever Lattices stand.
- **Nadir's economy:**
  - **Prism slag** is Lattice runoff that drains down from Zenith. Mirefen's marsh is made of it.
  - **The pits** are Ironreach's betting arenas.
  - **Sweetrot** is the street chem, and the Sump Barons control it.
  - **Information** is Nyx's trade.

  The City Below has no seat on the Zenith Council and pays Zenith's taxes anyway.
- **Money:** the Pokédollar (₽) is standard. Old-timers in Ossaria still quote prices in "bones", fossil-shell tokens.

## 9. Politics: the Accord versus the kingdoms

- **The tension in one line:** every king rules land with a Plate in it that they're forbidden to touch.
- **The Accord Council** (meets in Crownhaven): the Sovereign, the eighteen Wardens and the **Chancellor** (Ambrose Hollis), who runs the bureaucracy and the Wardguard budget. Votes are by Warden. Kings attend only as "honored observers", and they hate it.
- **Blocs** (from Act III, "Fault Lines"):
  - **Greenward** (limit extraction, oppose the Mandate): Juno, Marisol, Kaito, Corvin, Tamsin, Ysolde, Celestine.
  - **Concord bloc** (pro-Meridian): Magnus, Rook (until Act IV), Anja (until the Thaw), and Dorian (publicly, under protest).
  - **Sovereign loyalists:** Brann, Sigrun, Bastian, Sahar.
  - **Independents:** Odessa, Nyx, Ilsa.
- **The kingdoms' agendas:**
  - **Elowen** wants its Plates back as Crown property.
  - **Isenmar** wants heat and debt relief.
  - **Ashkaar** wants the Draco Plate and the Flame Plate under the Dominar, and Zenith humbled.
  - **Ossaria** sells whatever Meridian will buy.
  - **Antumbra** wants the Glass kept closed and Giratina undisturbed.
  - **Zenith** wants the Mandate everywhere.
  - **Nadir** wants to *exist*: recognition, a vote, clean air.
- **Outside:**
  - **The Concert of Regions** (the neighboring realms across the Hallow Strait) imposed the quarantine and will decide at this Festival whether to lift it or declare **Severance**, permanent abandonment. Its envoy is Delphine Okoro.
  - The **Veil Gate** through Antumbra opens to other *worlds*, and it has been sealed since AF 230. Since the Bleed and the Fault thinned the Glass, it **leaks**: travelers from other worlds slip into Velmora uninvited, and that is the basis of the optional cameo sidequests (`design/CAMEOS.md`).

## 10. Culture notes (for NPC writing)

- **Greeting:** "Light keep you." Reply: "And the One." Hollow sympathizers pointedly answer "And let it go."
- **The counting rhyme.** Jump-rope chant, known everywhere, in gym order. It is also a progression mnemonic:
  > *One for the stage and two for the weave,*
  > *three for the river that won't take its leave,*
  > *four for the lantern, five for the dune,*
  > *six for the fen and its poison-blue moon,*
  > *seven for embers, eight for the fist,*
  > *nine for the garden the frost never kissed,*
  > *ten for the lake and eleven for sky,*
  > *twelve for the dreamer who never asks why,*
  > *thirteen for stone and fourteen for bone,*
  > *fifteen for gears and sixteen unknown,*
  > *seventeen dusk and eighteen for wings —*
  > *and none for the cage, and none for the king.*

  (The Ebb "won't take its leave" because it always comes back: it runs both ways. "Sixteen unknown" because Underveil isn't on Accord maps. "None for the cage, and none for the king" refers to the Gilded Chain and the Gilded King, though no child knows that.)
- **Food:** Ferrow's Landing kelp-tea and bramble tarts. Briarwick honey-silk buns. Saltmere salt-caramel. Ebbmouth smoked eel. Lumen City Fizzbolt soda. Duneholt date-bread. Mirefen "bog coffee". Cindervault ember-roasted chestnuts. Rimeholt fermented fish (a dare). Gloaming dusk-honey, which supposedly lets you dream of the dead.
- **Holidays:** **Fallmorn**, the midsummer anniversary of the Prismfall, when families light eighteen candles and leave one holder empty. **The Festival** each decade.
- **Fashion tells:**
  - Elowen: leaf-green and embroidery.
  - Isenmar: furs and bone toggles.
  - Ashkaar: lacquered scale armor and red.
  - Ossaria: wraps, goggles, fossil jewelry.
  - Antumbra: veils, mirror-silver, mismatched gloves (left for the living, right for the dead).
  - Zenith: brass, glass, clean white.
  - Nadir: patched leathers, rebreathers, Hollow-grey.

## 11. Places

### Crownhaven (neutral; the Accord's capital; story-gated, Act V)
Built on the inner slopes of **the Crown**, the great caldera ring at the realm's center. Its eighteen gates face the eighteen Imprints. **Upper Crownhaven** is white stone, bell towers and the **Basilica of First Light**, and the **Temple-Arena of the Blessing** (rebuilt by Meridian as "the Meridian Prism Arena", and too new) rises above the **Court of Eight Pavilions**, where each kingdom's delegation and the Accord pitch their colors. **Lower Crownhaven** is the crowded crater-floor town that still has a scar: the **Glassfall Scar**, a district-sized stretch of glass where forty people were Fixed in AF 290. The Accord fenced it and planted a memorial garden around it, and nobody tends the garden. The **Concordance Spire**, the old Accord archive tower, becomes the post-game Battle Tower.

### Elowen, the Evergreen Crown
*The great forest at the heart of Velmora, from the south-west coast to the Gloamwood in the south-east, with Crownhaven at its centre. It borders every kingdom. An old monarchy under Queen Maelis Thorne. Wild types: Fairy, Grass, Bug, Normal. Character: storybook beauty with old rot underneath, a kingdom of fairy tales that are mostly lies.*
- **Ferrow's Landing** (start): Elowen's southern ferry dock on the Hallow Strait. A fishing village of tarred stilt-houses and drying kelp under a red-and-white lighthouse, where old Hesper Vane keeps the lamp and young Kestrel keeps out of sight. **Tidewatch Station**, Larch's field station and the Wayfarer Sanctuary, sits on the headland. Hale's Bramble berry farm is up the lane. The bronze **Accord plaque** at the dock reads "Eighteen in Concord, One in Keeping". After the last ferry, a Meridian customs gate stands beside it.
- **Saltmere** (Gym 1, Normal, `:GRAND_STAGE`): Elowen's chartered **free port** on a salt lake at the forest's coastal edge. Here the Blank Plate's Imprint is *ordinariness*: nothing strange ever happens in Saltmere, and Bleed never touches it. So the city built its own miracles, and it is Velmora's theater capital. The **Lantern Theatre** on the salt-flat promenade stages the Accord's official *Pageant of the Prismfall* every Fallmorn; in a Festival year the run opens in spring and plays through to Fallmorn. The **Pell Playhouse** in the Old Town is the gym. The free-port charter means Saltmere answers to no king, and the Queen's tax-barges are turned back at the breakwater by tradition. Saltmere keeps its own oil lamps and has refused Meridian's "free light" for the promenade, which Dorian calls part of the set. It is reached from Ferrow's Landing along the chalk coast (the Strand Road). Up the Weavers' Road toward Briarwick stands **Thistledown Manor**, home of a dead acting family who never stopped performing (the comic haunted house).
- **Briarwick** (Gym 2, Bug, `:SILKWOOD`): a weaving village inside the Gossamer Wood, where the Insect Plate makes Bug Pokémon spin silk that never tangles or rots. Houses are lashed between tree trunks, and the town looms clack all day. A shrine at the village edge honors **the Loyal Three**, who "drove the Gossamer Ogre from the wood". Children wear carved ogre masks at Fallmorn to scare it off. The road in is the Weavers' Road up the River Selvedge from Saltmere: the cloth goes down it, the salt comes up. The old forest road south to Ferrow's Landing is corded off, because the Glasswood has crossed it. The **Glasswood**, a crystallizing Bleed, is creeping toward the silk-moth groves from the east, along the line of Meridian's new Lattice.
- **Verdance** (Gym 9, Grass, `:FLOWER_GARDEN`): Elowen's royal seat. A green city of terraces and glasshouses where the Meadow Plate makes everything bloom constantly. **Verdance has never had an autumn.** It is a true castle-city: three rings of briar-grown curtain walls with thorn gatehouses enclose the Market Terraces, the Glasshouse District, the Cathedral of the Original One and the guild halls, and a tanners' slum sprawls along the river outside the walls. **Briar Keep** crowns the highest terrace, and Queen Maelis holds court in its great hall from a throne of living thorn, beneath galleries hung with three centuries of tapestries. Below it lies the Warden's **Heartgarden**, where the **Bride Tree** stands in permanent blossom. Underneath, the soil is grey and starving, and nothing rots, so nothing returns to the earth. The **Royal Chase**, the Queen's game reserve, sprawls outside the walls.
- **Gloaming** (Gym 17, Fairy, `:FAIRY_TALE`): the forest's oldest corner, deep in the south-east, where the Pixie Plate in the roots of the Great Tree holds the sky at permanent dusk. Lantern-moths, mushroom bridges, and a town of bargains and courtesies. Gloaming folk can't lie, age slowly, and keep the old songs. The approach is through the **Gloamwood**. By Act V, the dusk is spreading west across Elowen like a stain, toward Briarwick.

### Antumbra, the Kingdom Behind the Glass
*A mirrored realm behind Elowen's forest, entered through mirror gates such as Mirror Mere. The Reverse Kingdom is governed by the Veiled Synod under Archseer Imre Dask, and it is Giratina's domain. Wild types: Ghost, Psychic, Dark. Inverse rules (`:INVERSE`) hold in the borderlands: rivers run uphill, shadows fall toward the light, and type matchups flip.*
- **Lucent** (Gym 12, Psychic, `:MINDSCAPE`): a scholars' city that exists twice. The living city is reflected upside-down in the sky above it, and the Mind Plate lives in the reflection. The **Lucent Athenaeum** holds the Accord's archive, the sealed stacks, the Concordance original and the Secret Articles. The Accord holds its tribunals here, under Ilsa Marr's provostship. Lucent is Antumbra's seat. The Veiled Synod rules from the **Palace of Glass**, whose throne room floor is a still lake reflecting a second, inverted court where the Synod's reflections sit in council.
- **Gravenmoor** (Gym 14, Ghost, `:HOLLOW_CATHEDRAL`): a moorland cathedral town of fog, barrows and lanterns, where the Spooky Plate makes the dead *linger as echoes*. The **Hollow Cathedral** replays the last moments of anyone who died near it. **Vane Hall**, the Vane family manor and Kestrel's childhood home, stands empty on the hill, dust-sheeted and haunted. The **Lantern Hospice** is where Morwen died.

### Isenmar, the Frozen Sea
*The west and north-west coast, a sea frozen solid since AF 0 with a hard clan-kingdom built on it, under Thane Eirik Sorrel. Wild types: Water, Ice. Character: endurance, debt, and stubborn warmth.*
- **Ebbmouth** (Gym 3, Water, `:WATER_SURFACE`): Isenmar's southern port, where the River Ebb comes down from the Crownland downs to meet the open sea. The Splash Plate makes **the Ebb run both ways**, inland on the morning tide and out at night, and three centuries of tidal mills grew up around it. It is fog, gulls, smoked-eel stalls and union halls. The **Ebb Guild** elects the Warden. Meridian's tidal Lattice at the river mouth is draining the current, and the river has started to "stutter", stopping dead for minutes at a time. Nearby: **Ebb Hollow**, a Bleed zone where river-water hangs in the air in still sheets.
- **Rimeholt** (Gym 10, Ice, `:FROZEN_LAKE`): the northern ice-city built *on* **Lake Rime**, part of the frozen sea, frozen solid for three hundred years above the **Ice Vault**, where Kyogre sleeps. The houses are timber and whalebone, linked by sled-roads, and ice-fishers cut holes over a darkness that sometimes *breathes*. Meridian's Lattice heats the city and thins the ice. In Act III, a quarter of the city sinks. Rimeholt is Isenmar's seat. The Thane rules from **the Holdfast**, a fortress-port of black stone on the Hold-rock at the lake's heart: rime-white curtain walls, whalebone gatehouses, an ice-harbour for longships and sled-fleets, and a great hall whose hearth has burned for three hundred years.
- **Skaldfast** (the war, Acts III–V): Isenmar's oldest fort, the skalds' fastness, at the foot of the Mast Glacier on the edge of the bone sands, where the first thanes stood against the desert. In the war it is Isenmar's border fortress, besieged from Ch15 (14_WAR_AND_BLESSING).

### Zenith, the City Above
*A gleaming city-state on the southern plateau, all brass, glass, airship masts and progress. It is governed by the Zenith Council of founding houses, chaired by Aurelian Voss. Meridian's home. Wild types: Electric, Steel, Flying. Character: optimism with the bill sent downstairs.*
- **Lumen City** (Gym 4, Electric, `:NEON_GRID`): Zenith's capital district, a neon metropolis lit by the Zap Plate's free current plus Meridian's Lattice grid. Streets of advertising holograms, Rotom delivery drones and rooftop gardens. **Meridian Spire** rises from the cliff edge on the foundations of the old Aurelian sun tower, with its black **Dark Stone** core glowing in its heart. The **Lowlight** is the working-class district where the Spire's shadow falls. After the Shatter, part of it is glass. As Zenith's capital district, Lumen City climbs in towering tiers: the **Council Spire** where the Zenith Council sits, glass skybridges, airship docks, the Gilt Market, and at the bottom the lift-shafts down into Nadir.
- **Skyreach** (Gym 11, Flying, `:STORMFRONT`): Zenith's airship spire, a vertical city of docking masts and skybridges climbing into **the Stormfront**, the Sky Plate's permanent storm wall. Home of **the Gale Post** courier guild and of every smuggler worth hiring. Stormwatch Spire at its crown holds the relays that broadcast to the whole realm.
- **Cogsworth** (Gym 15, Steel, `:FACTORY`): Zenith's foundry district on the plateau's eastern edge, where the Iron Plate makes metal *eager* to be shaped. Smokestacks and clock-towers, three shifts a day. Magnus Strade's **Strade Works** builds everything from airship hulls to, secretly, the Arbiter Engine. The bathysphere works that build Dive suits for the drowned coast are here too.

### Nadir, the City Below
*The slums, sumps and old mine shafts beneath Zenith's cliffs, built in the caverns and in drowned Aurelia's upper towers. Zenith claims it, but it has no recognized government: Sump Barons, pit-bosses and Nyx Calloway run it. Wild types: Poison, Dark, Fighting. Character: rage, solidarity and the best street food in Velmora.*
- **Mirefen** (Gym 6, Poison, `:CORROSIVE_MIRE`): a stilt-town in the **runoff marsh** where Zenith's Lattice slag drains into the Toxic Plate's mire. The water glows faintly blue at night, which is the "poison-blue moon" of the rhyme. Fenfolk are tinted against poison and almost nothing else. **Corvin Mott's Infirmary** and its Quiet Ward stand on the highest stilts.
- **Ironreach** (Gym 8, Fighting, `:COLOSSEUM`): the pit-fighting quarter carved into the Aurel-era mine shafts. The Fist Plate makes people and Pokémon *strong*, so for centuries Nadir's miners have settled everything with their hands. The **Iron Pit**, a colosseum in a mined-out cavern, hosts the Iron Trial and the **Ironreach Open**. In Act II, the Accord's Conclave is held here in the shafts under Zenith's feet, to show the City Below the Accord still cares. It goes badly.
- **Underveil** (Gym 16, Dark, `:NEW_MOON`): the heart of Nadir, a city of lanterns and smugglers in the upper towers of drowned **Aurelia**, half-submerged in the sea-flooded **Sump** beneath the south cliffs. The Dread Plate keeps it in permanent night. It's not on Accord maps ("sixteen unknown"). Nyx Calloway's **Moon Market** trades in secrets, and she rules from the **Moon Court** in the topmost drowned tower. It is a vertical undercity of stairs, lifts and lantern-bridges stacked down the old palace's flanks. In Act III, the Fault drains the Sump overnight and exposes the Sun Court, the Gilded King's throne room, for the first time in 300 years.

### Ashkaar, the Dragonfire Dominion
*The eastern volcanic highlands, from Cinder Cape on the north-east coast down the Wyrmspine. A feudal warrior-kingdom of dragon-lords under Dominar Hrothgar Ashfall. Wild types: Fire, Dragon. Character: honor, oaths, and a war it badly wants.*
- **Cindervault** (Gym 7, Fire, `:VOLCANIC`): a forge-city inside the caldera of **the Cinderhorn**, where the Flame Plate keeps the magma at the perfect working heat. Terraced smithies, lava-channels for quenching and the **Hearthguild** of smiths. The guild vault holds the **Light Stone**, which the Dominar wants for his throne. Meridian's geothermal tap is overdrawing the caldera. Quench the Imprint and it becomes `:ASHEN_WASTE`. Cindervault is Ashkaar's seat: **Pyre Keep**, the Dominar's dragon-citadel of black basalt and red lacquer, stands on the caldera rim above the forge terraces, with dragon-roosts in its towers, a war-market in the lower bailey and the **Ember Throne** in a great hall over a channel of living magma.
- **Wyrmrest** (Gym 18, Dragon, `:DRAGONS_DEN`): the dragon-aerie fortress on the peaks that guard the **only overland pass into the Crown**. Dragon-riders patrol the **Wyrmscar Rift**. The Draco Plate sleeps in the Rift's heart under Sigrun Ashfall's guard, while her father's legions muster on the slopes below. By Act V they have gone to the war in Ossaria's sands.

### Ossaria, the Bone Sands
*The northern desert and canyon country along the north coast: fossil beds, caravan houses and deep mines. Ruled by Prince-Elector Idris Kassan, whom the caravan houses elect for life. Wild types: Ground, Rock. Character: patience, memory, and things buried for a reason.* Ossarian tomb-law says the sand keeps whoever dies on it, so the **bone-carters** bury strangers as carefully as their own. The western dunes lie between Isenmar and Ashkaar, who share no border, and the war of Acts III–V is fought there, on neutral ground (14_WAR_AND_BLESSING).
- **Duneholt** (Gym 5, Ground, `:DESERT`): a caravan city of sandstone terraces around a deep aquifer, which the Earth Plate keeps *inside the stone* so the desert above stays fertile. The dunes move, and every few years they uncover something. Sahar's **Dig Lab** (fossil revival) is here, and the **Ossuary of Dunes** (Unown ruins) is on the road out. Meridian's deep drills are collapsing the aquifer, the city's wells are sinking, and something enormous beneath the sand has started to warm. Duneholt is Ossaria's seat. The Prince-Elector's **Sandglass Palace** rises on the high mesa above the walled caravan districts and bazaars. Beyond the walls, the **Valley of Tombs** holds three centuries of caravan-princes in rock-cut halls.
- **Cragholm** (Gym 13, Rock, `:CRYSTAL_CAVERN`): a mining town carved down a canyon wall into crystal caverns. The Stone Plate grows gems the way Verdance grows flowers. It is lamplit galleries, lift-cages and the **deep seams**, where Bastian hides people the Accord and Meridian would rather didn't exist. Darken the caverns and they become `:DARK_CRYSTAL`.
