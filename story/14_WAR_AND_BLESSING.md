## PART A — ARCEUS'S BLESSING (the Festival reframed)

### What everyone believes
The Festival of Champions is the one sacred ceremony all seven kingdoms share. Every ten years each kingdom carries its Plates to Crownhaven and sends **two delegates** to fight before the Plates. The last one standing receives **Arceus's Blessing**:
1. **The Light.** The Crown is set on the victor inside the Concordance Ring, and the light of all eighteen Plates passes through them. The Choir teaches that this is Arceus touching one mortal.
2. **The Seat.** The Blessed holds the Sovereign Seat for the decade.
3. **The Boon.** The Blessed may speak **one decree that every crown must honor until the next Festival**: a border, a peace, a tax, a pardon. This is why kings care, and why they send their best. Maren's first Boon founded the Wardguard. Valen's three Boons: the Plate-tax ban (AF 270), the Marrowgate silence (AF 280, sealed), the Morwen verdict (AF 290, sealed).

### What is true
- **The Light is the Tuning.** "The Plates' light passing through the Blessed" is the bearer's body hushing the Plates. The Blessing shortens every life it touches. Nothing in 01 §5 changes except the name the public uses.
- **The Boon is real law** and was Maren's own invention: a reward large enough that the strongest person in the realm would always volunteer for a thing that kills them.
- **Valen** has been Blessed three times. He intends the PC to be the fourth name on the list and the last.
- **Voss** needs the Blessing moment itself: eighteen Plates converged, the Crown raised, a bearer in the Ring. The Arbiter Engine fires *during the Light*. He also wants Zenith's delegates to win the Boon as a fallback ("Mandate in perpetuity").
- **The Hymn of Return** read correctly: the last Blessed gives the Light *back*.

### Retcons (small)
- "Sovereign" = "the Blessed". The Coronation in Ch21 is "the Blessing". Arceus's speech gains one line: *"They call this my blessing. I have never once been asked."*
- The Good ending adds a Boon beat: with the Plates gone, the PC still gets one decree. **CHOICE `:ch21_boon` *(proposed)*:** end the war's reparations / seat Nadir on every council / dissolve the Seat itself.
- Entrants are **delegations**, not a free draw (below). The bowl still decides pairings.

## PART B — THE ASHKAAR–ISENMAR WAR

### Why they fight (and why it isn't a detour)
- **Isenmar's heat is Ashkaar's fire.** Meridian's heat-towers in Rimeholt are fed by Prism Cells charged at the **Cindervault geothermal tap**. Ashkaar's Flame Imprint is being drained to keep Isenmar's children warm. Meridian sold it to both crowns as two unrelated contracts.
- **Old grievance: the bone sands between them.** Fire and ice share no border (WORLD.md §10). Isenmar holds the west, Ashkaar the east, and Elowen and Ossaria lie between. The one ground both can reach is **Ossaria's western dunes**: Isenmar comes down the Mast Glacier, Ashkaar comes west through the Cinderglass Barrens. Isenmar's sled-caravans and Ashkaar's riders both cross those dunes to the salt and glass markets, both claim the old caravan road and its wells under treaties with long-dead Electors, and they have skirmished there for a century. Ossaria signed neither treaty and honors neither.
- **Voss wants the war.** Two opposed Imprints colliding at scale is the best calibration data the Engine can get short of the Festival. Meridian arms both sides with Prism weapons and logs every clash. The war also keeps two crowns too busy to vote against the Mandate.
- **The Bleed:** wherever the Fire and Ice Imprints meet, the sand between them turns to steam-glass, a Bleed zone. The front line is a Bleed line, the Steamline, and only the PC can cross it.
- **The legendaries:** Kyogre (woken by the Thaw) and Reshiram (the Light Stone) become the kingdoms' banners, and the climax is their clash: a sea dragged into a desert against a fire that turns it to glass. Ossaria has seen this once before. Its Unown records call it **the Year of Two Weathers** (01 §2), and the Dry Tide west of Duneholt is the seabed it left behind.

### Ossaria: the ground the war is fought on
Two foreign armies fight on Ossarian sand, and Ossaria pays for it. This is how the Bone Sands answer.
- **Prince-Elector Kassan sells the battlefield.** When the Dominar closes the eastern passes (Ch8), Kassan declares Ossaria neutral, then rents the war a place to happen. Under **the Elector's Lease** the Thane gets the western edge (Skaldfast's "forward works" on the Rimed Sands), the Dominar gets the eastern dunes for his camp, and Meridian gets the road between them for its "neutral logistics". Three treasuries pay him rent. The one clause he enforces is that no army comes within a day's ride of Duneholt's Sandgate. It is Meridian's trick with the heat, done with sand: sell the same thing to both sides. Kassan, in Duneholt's Hall of Audience (07, Ch14 transit): *"The Bone Sands have buried every army that ever marched into them. I only charge for the plot."*
- **The caravan houses turn their backs on him.** The western dunes are the caravan road: its wells, its salt pans, the glass market. The war closes all of it. The caravan-mothers elected Kassan for life and can't unseat him, so they ignore him. They guide refugees east to Cragholm, sell bread to both armies at war prices, and lead Marisol's relief sledges to the edge of the Steamline (`:war_convoy`). Through Acts IV–V Duneholt's caravans leave by the east gate, one house at a time.
- **The bone-carters bury everyone.** Ossarian tomb-law says the sand keeps whoever dies on it, so no army takes its dead home. At dusk the **bone-carters** of Duneholt and Vigil walk out between the lines under a white bone-banner, and both armies hold fire, because both want their dead buried properly. It's the one thing the war agrees on, and the only flag both sides let pass: Anja's refugee column comes east under it (07, Ch14). The carters can't go into the Steamline's glass, so they ask the PC to bring out the ones inside it (optional, from their camp on the Dry Tide, the old seabed west of Duneholt). They witness the `:war_prisoner` exchange at a caravan well, where Ossarian guest-law forbids blood, and after the climax they are the first people onto the glass.
- **The Wardens.** Sahar has read the war before anyone else. The Ossuary's mural of the red titan and the blue one tearing the land (Ch6) shows the western dunes' coastline, and after Groudon's fight in the Undersands she says it aloud (Ch14): "The sand remembers the sea. It doesn't want it back." Bastian shelters both kingdoms' refugees in his seams, and the Ossarian well-folk the armies drove off their wells.
- **What it changes** (no new keys; this is how the war keys below read on Ossarian ground): an *occupation* leaves a foreign army on Ossarian sand, so the land with a boot on its neck is the one kingdom that never fought. *Reparations* make Kassan bill both crowns "for the sand". With *mercy*, or with the war stopped, the caravans are back on the western road within the month. In the epilogue Kassan is arrested (Good), with the Lease read out among the charges, or knighted by Meridian (Bad), with the Lease renewed in Meridian's name.

### The Battle of the Glass Dunes (Ch19)
**Setup.** The front is in Ossaria's western dunes (WORLD.md §9, WORLD_MAP.md §7). Isenmar holds **Skaldfast**, its border fortress on Ossaria's west edge at the foot of the Mast Glacier, and has dug white trench-lines across **the Rimed Sands** east of it, sand its host has frozen hard. Ashkaar's legions hold the **Ashkaar Siege Camp** in the bone sands further east, supplied from **Cinder Cape Warfort** on Ashkaar's north-east coast through the Cinderglass Barrens and Glasswake. The Bleed strip of steam-glass where the two Imprints meet is **the Steamline**, and south of it, between the hosts, lie the dunes both sides want: the caravan road and its wells, no-man's-land since Ch14. Sigrun's trial and oath (08, Ch19) happen first, at Wyrmrest. Then word comes that Kyogre has risen from the Ice Vault and is dragging a flood-sea across the desert, and that her father has carried the Light Stone to the front (if it hung in Wyrmrest's Rift-shrine, his riders took it while Sigrun was in the Rift with the PC). Sigrun flies the PC there overnight and sets them down at dawn on the Dry Tide, by the bone-carters' camp a mile behind the lines. The climax is fought on **the Glass Dunes** between the two hosts, where the flood meets Reshiram's fire and the sand turns to glass.

**The flood-sea.** Primal Kyogre leaves the Ice Vault under the frozen sea, swims east into the open water off Ossaria's coast, and comes ashore at the western dunes, dragging the Northern Sea in behind it over the Rimed Sands and down into the dunes. For one morning the Dry Tide's old seabed is full again. Reshiram answers it, and its Blue Flare turns wet sand to glass wherever it passes, so the beam clash happens over glassing dunes. If `:war_legend` is *neither*, neither legend comes and there is no flood-sea (see the battle, below).

**The six war choices** (keys and effects: NARRATIVE_KEYS §4.3; this is their staging):
1. **`:war_warning`** (Ch11, before the Thaw). From the top of **Frostfang Pass** on a hard, clear morning, the PC can see north-east across the Mast Glacier to the bone sands: cookfire smoke along the horizon and a dragon-column circling over it. The Dominar's legions are already on Ossarian sand, and nobody in Isenmar knows. Warn Isenmar, warn Ashkaar that they've been seen, warn both through Valen, or stay silent.
2. **`:war_convoy`** (Ch14). Marisol's siege-runners have bought food in Duneholt's bazaar (the caravan-mothers sell to anyone) and need the Unimprinted to walk the convoy the last mile. The caravan-mothers lead the sledges west across the sands from Duneholt to the edge of the Steamline. From there the PC walks point through the steam-glass, the Bleed quieting around them, and the sledges follow in their footsteps into Isenmar's lines and on to Skaldfast as the siege closes: *relief* (food), *arms* (harpoon-guns), or *refuse* (the caravan-mothers take the food back to Duneholt's refugees).
3. **`:war_prisoner`** (Ch15–16). Anja went west from Cragholm to Skaldfast after Ch14 and was taken in a sortie on the Rimed Sands. Ashkaar holds her in the Siege Camp; the Thane holds the Dominar's nephew in Rime Keep. Odessa flies the PC over the lines by Gale Post, and from there it's a short walk across the sands. *Free* Anja by walking her out through the Steamline, broker an *exchange* at a caravan well between the lines under the bone-carters' banner (no blood at a well), or *leave* it. This is Anja's extra pre-Festival scene: in the cage she says, "Tell my brother I'd have signed for the heaters again."
4. **`:war_legend`** (Ch17–18). Each side begs the PC to bring its legend to the front: *calm Kyogre* in the Ice Vault (Isenmar's ask: quiet it enough to follow the skalds' horns east), *wake Reshiram* early with the oath (Ashkaar's ask), or *neither*. A legend on the sands calls its opposite, as in the Year of Two Weathers, so *calm* or *wake* puts both legends at the climax (a calmed Kyogre fires only when Reshiram does). *Neither* leaves both asleep and the dunes dry. It is the only value that keeps them away (the cutscene plugin's `legends: false`).
5. **`:war_climax`** (Ch19, MAJOR). On the Glass Dunes, with the flood coming in: *stand between*, *back Ashkaar* or *back Isenmar*.
6. **`:war_terms`** (Ch19 aftermath, only if one side won). At the armistice table in Skaldfast's great hall, with Kassan seated at the end of it uninvited ("Nobody signs anything about my sand without me"), the PC argues for *mercy* (both armies off the sands), *reparations* (the loser pays the winner, and Kassan bills them both) or *occupation* (the winner garrisons the western dunes and the loser's road into them: Skaldfast and the Mast Glacier road if Ashkaar won, Glasswake and the Cinderglass Barrens if Isenmar won, and Kassan extends the Lease for ever).

**The four outcomes:**
- **`WAR_STOPPED`** (stood between, and both armies have seen enough of the PC to trust them: War Trust ≥ 4). Both lines hold fire. The beams meet over the PC and part. The flood goes back to the sea with Kyogre, and the glass stops where it is.
- **`WAR_BOTH_BROKEN`** (stood between without that trust). Nobody listens. The clash detonates over the dunes and glasses both front lines where they stand. Both kingdoms forfeit a Festival seat.
- **`WAR_ASHKAAR_WON`** / **`WAR_ISENMAR_WON`.** The loser's host breaks, Isenmar back behind Skaldfast's walls or Ashkaar back over the Siege Camp's earthwork, and the winner's banners go up over it. The loser forfeits a seat. *Occupation* terms poison the Good ending: Arceus won't bless a land with a boot on its neck, and this boot stands on two, the loser's and Ossaria's.

**The battle.** Playable set pieces around the cutscene: from the Dry Tide, a gauntlet through the lines of the side you didn't choose (Isenmar's trench-lines on the Rimed Sands, or the Siege Camp's earthworks; both, by way of the Steamline, if you stand between), a partner battle with Sigrun or Marisol, then the boss fight **as written for Reshiram** in 08, reframed: the PC fights whichever legendary is about to fire on the other army (Reshiram by default; **Kyogre** if backing Ashkaar). It is fought on the Glass Dunes' own field, `:DESERT` until the flood comes in and then changed by the flood and the fire (the fields team owns those transitions). The PC's oath (at Wyrmrest, or spoken early with `:wake_reshiram`) is what the Light Stone was waiting for: it wakes in the Dominar's hand here (WAR-1 shot 3). If `:war_legend` kept both legends away, the clash is between the Wardens' aces instead, Sigrun's Salamence against Anja's Mamoswine or Marisol's Gyarados, and the Light Stone stays dark in the Dominar's hand: Reshiram's fight happens at Wyrmrest as 08 wrote it, after the truce, when the stone comes home.

**The climax** is the pixel-animated cutscene **WAR-1 "The Beam Clash on the Glass Dunes"** (`13_CUTSCENES.md`): Kyogre's Origin Pulse, fired out of the flood-sea it dragged into the desert, meets Reshiram's Blue Flare over the flooded dunes. Where they meet, sand and flood-water turn to steam-glass and a Bleed dome swells over both armies. The PC's shard (and the two Wardens who love each side) ends it.

**Aftermath.**
- Meridian observers on a sandstone mesa behind the lines pack up their instruments before the steam clears. Larch, by radio: "That wasn't a battle to them. That was a *reading*." Field Notes logs the Engine's calibration jumping.
- Sigrun and Anja (or Marisol) stand on the glass between the armies and call the truce: the war will be settled by the Boon. The rulers agree because their legendaries have both left: Reshiram to Sigrun, Kyogre back to the sea, its flood draining out over the dunes behind it.
- The bone-carters walk out onto the glass under a new white banner. The Glass Dunes stay glass: a steam-glass plain across the caravan road, with both armies' glassed dead standing in it. Ossaria claims it under tomb-law, the newest necropolis in the Bone Sands and the only one with no Ossarians in it.
- Then come the Dominar's petition in his command pavilion (08, `:ch19_dominar`) and, if one side won, the armistice in Skaldfast (`:war_terms`). Sigrun flies the PC back to Wyrmrest for Gym 18.

### What the war changes later
- **Consent:** side-taking adds refusals (Brann or Anja/Marisol). Standing between adds none.
- **Hijack montage:** if the truce held without a victor, Ashkaar's riders hold the sky and Isenmar's ice-riders hold the lake shore, together.
- **Endings:** Homecoming's Boon can end reparations, and with them an occupation of Ossaria's sand. The Gilded Chain: Meridian annexes both exhausted kingdoms and buys the Lease from Kassan. Hollow Spring: the war restarts with no Plates and no fire, on the same sand.
